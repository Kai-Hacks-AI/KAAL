# Retro

## Learned

The smallest Intent capability is mostly a discipline and hardly a mechanism. The part that must be exact is small: an Intent is identified by its bytes, and everything else (what is wanted, why, where the boundaries lie, whether it has drifted into how) is judgement the script cannot make and must not pretend to. I learned that Review already was the reusable half: because it names a result and an identity and knows no kind, "reviewable beyond Changing" cost nothing. I also learned how small the consumption can be while the fixed target stays intact: the strongest thing I could add was to give the existing sentence something exact to hold against, not a new mechanism.

## Liked

The shape of the recent Skills, which let me spend the thinking on what Intent is rather than on packaging. The acceptance idea of proving the identity equals Sealing's, so no second meaning exists, and of running a real Review round over an Intent through the delivered scripts. The Reviewer's finding was concrete and reproduced with a tab character, cheap to fix, and stopping at the empty seat until Kai assigned a Reviewer kept the record honest.

## Lacked

I wrote the Architecture as "a space" and the code as "whitespace" and only a Reviewer saw the difference; I lacked a habit of testing each sentence of my own Architecture as a refusal case before review. I also lacked a way to see how the Intent that Kai hands over relates to the copy in work/01-intent.md: the byte-for-byte rule is stated in words and held only by reading, since nothing compares the two. Sealing the Node before review also left no clean way to change its wording if the Reviewer had asked.

## Longed

That change-state could name the established Intent's identity and show when work/01-intent.md departs from it, if Kai decides the evaluator should know about Intent. I longed for a place where an established Intent lives before a Change takes it up, so the thing handed over has an identity before anyone works from it. And I wanted the conversation that produces an Intent to be tried with the Owner on a real want, not only described in a Skill text; I could only write down what I believe helps.
