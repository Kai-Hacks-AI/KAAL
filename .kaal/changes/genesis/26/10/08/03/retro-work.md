# Retro

## Learned

That where the engine sits and where the Work sits are two questions I had fused. My first model made root-level changes/ the answer and spent its care on how to get there, when Kai's Intent was about independence, not a destination. The machinery already treats the Record as one directory argument and never looks at the engine; only defaults and the controls tied it to .kaal. I also learned that a single Record parameter is not enough once Records move: the baseline and the candidate must be resolved separately, and the baseline from the target revision, or the candidate can hide history by its own locator.

## Liked

That the demonstrations used the real 24 Changes and 47 seals and the existing predicate, so the claims about identity rest on something that ran and not on reading. The wrong cases next to the right ones (empty baseline preserved, empty candidate refused, hidden baseline preserved) made the design argument visible. The independent Reviewer's finding was exact and reproducible, and the Owner's clarification cut the Work down to its Intent instead of piling onto it.

## Lacked

I committed to a model, root changes/, before asking what in the Intent it served, and only the Owner's clarification corrected that. I lacked a habit of testing my own design sentence against the case that would break it: the single-locator claim was wrong in a way a two-row scratch check would have shown before review. I also lacked any run of the product controls or the full lifecycle outside .kaal, so R8 is argued and partly demonstrated, not met, and the Work says so.

## Longed

That the controls could be told which Records they judge, with the baseline's locations taken from the target revision, so this question could be answered by running them and not by prototype. I longed for a stated place where a mode declares its Record locations, and for a way to name an External Subject that a Change can refer to without KAAL interpreting it; I could only leave both as forks for the Owner.
