# Retro

## Learned

I learned that extending a canonical round to accept historical accounts changes more than presentation. The first parser accepted a second Findings section while retaining the first, and Review and Changing recognized different sets of metadata lines. The useful review questions were whether a file could contain unresolved findings while reporting convergence, and whether one consumer could accept what the other rejected. The green suite did not answer those questions until I tried the counterexamples.

Reading the Worker's retrospective clarified how the duplicate-section defect arose from accommodating real older rounds. That explains the choice without making the resulting ambiguity acceptable.

## Liked

The fixed Intent, explicit Requirements and exact Work identity gave the review a clear boundary. I could report two reproduced failures without turning the Change into a redesign of ROWING or a decision about Roles.

The Worker resolved both findings within that boundary, documented the text constraints and preserved the evaluator. The next round could verify the original counterexamples and the permitted indented quotations directly. Keeping the two rounds immutable made the change in judgment inspectable.

## Lacked

I lacked a natural place in the generated converged round for the disposition of earlier findings and the verification evidence. The writer supplies Reviewer and Findings text, with Findings fixed to None. on convergence; I carried the assessment beside the authority statement in Reviewer. The checker accepts additional account sections, but the writer does not expose those. This made my report less clearly separated than the existing rounds with dedicated assessment and evidence sections.

## Longed

I would like to review an actual Requirement, architectural result or operational outcome with this capability outside Changing KAAL. That would let me observe whether the result identity, review scope and authority supplied by the invoking context remain understandable without the Change lifecycle providing them.

I would also like later uses to demonstrate when a review adds useful judgment and when another round adds no information. The Owner's wish to keep Review optional matters to that experience: reuse should earn its place through the results it improves.
