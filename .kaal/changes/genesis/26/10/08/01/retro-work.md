# Retro

## Learned

The smallest Review is smaller than I first expected: a round and the question of convergence. What looked like a design question (how Changing KAAL consumes it) dissolved once the round form was the one ROWING already described; the only generic part was the name of the result. I also learned that a lenient check on real, historical files is a trap: the first strict form rejected rounds the repository already holds, and the leniency I added then opened the duplicate-section hole the Reviewer found.

## Liked

Having the evaluator's own source to read, so compatibility could be proved by running it on rounds Review wrote rather than argued. The Reviewer's findings were concrete and reproduced, which made them cheap to fix, and stopping at the empty seat instead of reviewing myself kept the record honest.

## Lacked

A single definition of a round that both Review and change-state read. I parsed the same two lines in two places and could only reduce the risk with a test, which is exactly where the second finding came from. I also lacked a way to see, before pushing, what real rounds look like in aggregate; I found their variety by failing a test.

## Longed

That change-state asked Review for convergence the way it asks Sealing for identities, so the round has one parser, if Kai decides an installed prerequisite is acceptable. I also longed for the Reviewer's reserved-line and duplicate-part checks to be written as questions at the start, before I chose leniency.
