# Investigation — Inner Loop and HOW Escalation

Grounded in `kaal/genesis` at 7613ad9 (#73 merged), `packages/kaal-review`, `packages/kaal-intent`, `packages/kaal-changing`, the closed Changes in `.kaal/changes/`, and what Codex has actually posted on PRs #72 and #73. Each answer says what was read and what is only recommended.

## 1. What is already there

- **Review gives agreement its mechanical core.** `review.mjs converged <dir> <identity>` exits 0 only when rounds `01.md …` are all valid rounds with no gap and the latest says `converged` and names exactly that identity. A round's `Result:` and its subject identity are mechanical; its Reviewer statement and Findings are free text. Review says of its own statement that it "is something an owner can inspect and is not proof". `rounds()` and `parse()` are exported from the same file.
- **Intent gives the subject its identity.** The identity of an Intent is the SHA-256 of its exact bytes (`intent.mjs identity`). A revised draft is therefore a different identity by construction. Intent decides nothing about when, whether or by whom it is reviewed (its step 6).
- **#73 already defined this review.** Its lens table has "Intent adequacy": internal, subject an Intent, standard the Way of Working of `Intent`, expectation "Is the Intent correctly described?", discipline Review, applies before the Intent is established, "information unless a Reviewer holds an assigned seat; never in `review/`". "Describe Intent ↔ Review Intent" is that lens run in a loop. A Process is a specialized Skill that pins what it composes by `{name, id}` and declares its prerequisites in `compatibility`; Process may depend on Changing, never the reverse.
- **Seats are settled.** `rowing.md`: the Worker may "deliver the Intent, the Work, its evidence and review requests to an actor already assigned to the Reviewer seat, and respond to findings, including by prompting or resuming that Reviewer's session"; it may not assign, select or control the Reviewer. Process determines the turn, authority assigns the seat. So `@codex review` posted by the Worker is legitimate **only** when Codex already holds the seat by the Owner's grant.
- **What Codex actually emits** (read on #72): a PR review by `chatgpt-codex-connector[bot]`, state `COMMENTED`, whose body carries `Reviewed commit: <sha>`, plus one inline thread per finding, each opening with a priority badge (`P2 Badge`) and a one-line title. Its own boilerplate says: "If Codex has suggestions, it will comment; otherwise it will react with 👍". I have not yet seen the 👍 path on this repository; it is the Reviewer's published behaviour, not something I verified.

## 2. The seven questions

**Q1. What constitutes agreement between Worker and Reviewer?** Two layers, which the first design merged and the Owner's review separated. *Reported*: a mechanical fact, no judgment about prose: *the latest recorded round was reported by the actor the Owner's grant names as Reviewer (not the Worker), says `converged`, and names the identity of the subject as it stands now (recomputed from the file's bytes), and no revision of the subject follows it.* The Worker's side of the agreement is that it put that exact subject forward and did not leave a finding unanswered; it is evidenced by the subject being the one the round names. "As it stands now" matters: Review's own rule is that changing the result afterwards un-converges it. *Verified*: that report, that grant and every direction are shown by the host's records to be made by who they claim (`state --host`); only then is it AGREED. A record the Worker can write is only ever reported, and the Process says so.

**Q2. What constitutes failure of the inner loop?** The loop fails when it can no longer reach agreement *without someone with more authority than the Worker deciding*. That is a closed list of conditions on the evidence (§3), not a mood. Failure is not "the Reviewer keeps finding things": a Reviewer finding new real problems each round is the loop working.

**Q3. Progress, stagnation, oscillation.** Only the bytes of the subject and the order of rounds are mechanical, so these three are defined on identities:
- *Progress*: after a round with findings, the next subject has an identity not seen before in the loop's current window.
- *Stagnation*: after a round with findings, the Worker puts forward the same identity again (it asks for review without having changed anything). A disagreement with a finding looks exactly like this, which is why it is an escalation and not something the Worker may settle.
- *Oscillation*: an identity recurs after a different one in between (A, B, A): the Worker is undoing its own revision, typically because two findings pull in opposite directions.
Not mechanical, so not used: whether a finding is resolved, whether findings are getting fewer, whether a revision is any good. Counting bullets in Findings would interpret free text. A *byte* change that is not an improvement (whitespace thrash) is progress by this definition; the Owner-granted budget (§3, `ceiling`) is what bounds that.

**Q4. Which conditions require HOW?** Derived from the evidence, each with a stable code (full table in `04-architecture.md`): `grant` (no valid authority was given), `ceiling` (the grant's budget of findings rounds is spent), `unrevised` (stagnation), `oscillation`, `contradiction` (the Reviewer reports differently about identical bytes; reachable only after a human continued an `unrevised` or `oscillation`), `seat` (a round not attributed to the granted Reviewer, or attributed to the Worker), `evidence` (the record is malformed, gapped, or no longer matches the bytes it names), `provenance` (the host's records do not bear out what the record claims). Round count is one of eight, not the criterion. History supports not leaning on it: 07/06 took six rounds and 07/07 holds eight (four of them Worker-side probes, not Reviewer evidence), while 08/01, 08/02, 08/03 and 09/01 took two. A count of 2 and a count of 6 can both be a healthy loop.

**Q5. How does human direction return authority?** As an artifact in the loop's own record, so that it is traceable and the state is still derived: a direction names the reason code it answers (it is refused if that is not the reason in force), says `continue` or `stop`, and for `continue` grants a new budget. A direction opens a new *window*: criteria are evaluated on rounds after it, so the loop returns to the inner loop with authority exactly as large as the human gave, and no larger. The Worker cannot write a direction that answers a reason it did not have, and nothing in the Worker's seat can grant one: the script cannot see who runs it (see §3), which is why §4 records the limit rather than hiding it.

**Q6. Consuming review evidence without taking free text as authority.** Split a round into what a script may read and what only a reader may read. *Read by script*: the `Result:` line, the subject name and identity, the round's position, the Reviewer actor line (compared for equality with the grant, never interpreted), and the identity of the subject files. *Read by people (and the next Worker)*: the Reviewer statement and the Findings. The script never branches on anything inside Findings. Review's existing `parse()` is reused, so there is still one parser of a round.

**Q7. Reuse.** From `kaal-review`: the round form, its parser, exact-identity convergence. From `kaal-intent`: `check` (is this file an Intent) and `identity`. From #73: the lens "Intent adequacy" as the review's subject, standard and expected examination, and the shape of a Process (specialized Skill, pins, `compatibility`). Not reused: `change-state.mjs` (it is about a Change and its order, and is Changing's), and no change to Review, Intent or Changing.

## 3. Findings that shaped the design

1. **Evidence has to be written down for state to be derived.** A refused action leaves no trace, so a refusal cannot be the evidence of a stagnation. The record must take the Worker's re-submission and the *derivation* must judge it. Hence: commands append; `state` judges.
2. **Where the authority to continue comes from.** Kai's ruling (Change 07/08): process determines the turn, authority assigns the seat. A loop that runs "while authorized" needs the authorization to be a thing the Process can read: an Owner's *grant* naming the Worker, the Reviewer and a budget. The Process reads it; it never writes it.
3. **KAAL cannot see who ran a script.** Review's statement is not proof and KAAL's checks "see artifacts and their order, never who wrote them". The honest consequence: the loop's strongest guarantee is that *what the Agent can do through the Process's commands* is bounded and its record is checkable by anyone with the grant and the host's provenance (review id, commit). Hand-editing the record is possible; it is detected when it breaks the derivation (a gap, a changed identity), not when it forges a consistent one. This is the same residual limit Review states.
4. **The host edge is real and small.** Codex answers on GitHub; the loop's record is files; KAAL "knows nothing of Git, GitHub, any process or any host". Someone has to carry a bot's review into a round. §4 of the architecture names who and why that is a transcription and not a judgment, and offers the alternatives.
5. **History would have been caught correctly.** Applied to the 07/07 rounds (Review form, readable): round 04 says `converged` on `be5d15d0…` and round 05 reviews the same identity as `findings`, i.e. a `contradiction`. Rounds 01 to 04 were Worker-side probes, which Kai's ruling already excluded; with a grant naming the Reviewer they would be refused as `seat` before the contradiction mattered. 08/01, 08/02, 08/03, 09/01 are findings-then-converged on distinct identities: none of the identity conditions would fire (they predate the grant and the `Actor:` line, so `seat` and `grant` cannot be applied to them).

## 4. What this investigation does not settle

- Whether the Worker's transcription of a bot's review into a round is acceptable as the host edge (architecture Q1).
- Whether a loop belongs inside a Change record or beside the draft Intent (architecture Q4).
- Whether Codex's 👍 is observable the way its boilerplate says (live trial, `05-evidence.md`, if it can be run on this PR).
