# Retro

## Learned

I learned that independent review was most useful when I reproduced a claimed authority transition against the shipped script. The original passing tests shared the implementation’s assumptions; stale authentic reports, hidden human directions and reopened stops exposed gaps that a larger test count alone would not have revealed. Scope review also changed my understanding of the loop: reconsideration of a grounded disposition can be progress without changing the subject bytes.

## Liked

I liked the concrete exchange between findings and focused refusal evidence. Each immutable round named the observed Work, and later rounds stated which earlier findings were resolved. Keeping the original out-of-scope observation while testing challenge, deferral and disagreement preserved a review history I could inspect without requiring the Worker to implement everything I mentioned.

## Lacked

I lacked an independently authenticated live path to AGREED. The real Codex interaction was useful evidence, but the Worker-authored grant and externally assembled host records prevented it from demonstrating the whole trust chain. I also initially reported findings only in chat rather than recording the required round; Kai’s instruction to follow protocol corrected that process gap.

## Longed

I would like future review to start with adversarial authority and recovery examples, before a state machine is treated as complete. I would also like a verifier outside the Worker to supply real host evidence and a second subject to exercise the composition boundary, so that both provenance and reuse are learned from operation rather than inferred from this first realization.
