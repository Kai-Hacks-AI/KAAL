# Retro

## Learned

That a protected boundary is not blocked from carrying a record of itself: the gate that looked like a wall for a one-file bridge was asking for a Change, and the isolation rule had already been adjusted to let one travel with its boundary. I had read the first red result as a gap in the design when it was the design working. I also learned how small a compatibility step can be when the evaluator reports its own facts: reading one printed line was enough to serve two evaluators with one fixture.

## Liked

That the bridge could stay nine lines and leave every case of the test with its name and its expectation, so there was nothing new to defend. Running the same fixture against both evaluators and seeing the same cases pass made the claim checkable instead of argued.

## Lacked

A way to see, before opening the change, that a support edit needs its own Change. I only found out from the gate, and asked a question the lineage had already answered. A note in the Skill about when a boundary change carries its record would have saved a round.

## Longed

For the second perspective of this Change to come from outside the Work, supplied by someone who did not do it. I have written only my own retrospective and stopped, because filling the observer's seat myself is exactly what the process is trying to prevent.
