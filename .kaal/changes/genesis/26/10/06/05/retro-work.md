# Retro

## Learned

That the direction rule did most of the design. Once the extension could only reach KAAL as a process and the only policy left to it was which paths may travel together, the controls became a baseline plus a command, and the shell scripts' cases ported almost verbatim. What surprised me was that running the real closed records as test subjects found a flaw in my own fixture, an open Change of this branch counting as a second admission, which a hand-made fixture would have hidden.

## Liked

That the KAAL-backed tests use the repository's own records and real edits, so nothing can pass because a simulator agreed with itself, and that the extension has no import of KAAL and a test keeping its sources free of process words, so the boundary is enforced and not only intended.

## Lacked

A way to prove the extension and the shell scripts agree other than porting their cases. The cases agree, but the three KAAL-backed controls are wider than the shell ones on purpose, installed seals instead of Core's, and nothing records that comparison except this Work.

## Longed

For the cutover to follow at once, so that two implementations of isolation do not live side by side for longer than one Change, and for pushed seal immutability to be decided on its own so these controls can grow to enforce it.
