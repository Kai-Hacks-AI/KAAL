# Retro

## Learned

That the three concepts in the Intent (known, reachable, all) did not need a mechanism so much as a refusal to store one: giving each a different home, and storing only the attempt, made the registry unnecessary. I also learned that the capability could not stay inside its own package: a real collection under .kaal made the installation check fail, which no acceptance test of the capability alone would have shown, so I only found it by running the real thing against this repository. Reconciling with the admitted Incident and Request showed how little I needed to assume: one list of two directory names, and no knowledge of a carrier's form. My own error is part of what I learned: I took an independent Reviewer to be something I could create, and the boundary between the Worker and the review side is exactly that I cannot.

## Liked

That the script reaches nothing and the agent brings the exposure to it, so adapter neutrality is a property of the code and not a promise in prose: there is no place for a host to leak in. I liked that a record is write-once and that a racing or refused attempt removes only what it wrote, and that an unreached client is a first-class record with a reason, which keeps partial visibility visible. The independent review was valuable: its dangling-link finding was a real gap in check that my own probing had missed, and both its rounds anchored findings to the Requirements.

## Lacked

An independent Reviewer from the start; I lost time and a round of trust by appointing my own, and had to withdraw it. I also lacked the carriers' form while building: I had to assume a location and rebuild once Incident and Request landed, and the Change address moved under me twice. Recording the green full suite was blocked by the process itself, since editing the sealed-to-be evidence would have reopened review, so the final result lives in a PR comment and not in the Work.

## Longed

For a collection to be run against a real client with the Owner's word, so the first evidence of actual use, rather than hand-built test layouts, tells us whether the one list of carried places and the reached or unreached distinction are enough. I would also like the question of whether a collection should ever be sealed, and what an Owner-side reading of collected carriers looks like, to be taken up as its own Change and not folded into this one.
