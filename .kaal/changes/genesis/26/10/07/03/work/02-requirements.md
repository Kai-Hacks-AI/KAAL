# Requirements

R1. **The handoff is the authority to work.** The human and the Owner describe the Intent together. That described Intent, handed to the Worker, is the authority to work. The Work record represents it; no filename or file shape is what makes the handoff authoritative, and how each stage is represented is left open. The Worker carries the Work through its progression (Intent described, Requirements defined, Architecture designed, code developed, done, defects detected) without returning to the Owner for permission at each step. A stage being complete is not a stop.

R2. **A transition is not a permission.** Where the process says a next act is legal, and the actor holds the role of that act, the actor does it. Neither the order of acts nor the end of one of them calls for another "go".

R3. **The Worker stops only at a boundary that is real.** The Worker continues until the process requires Review or a genuine authority boundary is met, which is a decision that is genuinely the Owner's and unresolved. Completion of a stage is not, by itself, a handoff. Where Review falls within the progression is not settled by this Change: boundaries that add value and those that are ceremony are learned from ordinary Changes. Nothing else is a stop.

R4. **Roles are not actors.** One actor may hold several roles and several actors may share one Change. Role boundaries are ordered knowledge boundaries: they fix whose perspective an act comes from and what it may draw on, not who must be asked. The process asks for independence of the Reviewer; it does not make an actor switch merely to look independent, and it names no number of actors.

R5. **Owner authority stays explicit.** Describing the Intent is co-work between the human and the Owner and is not solely the Owner's act; once handed to the Worker it is neither owned nor revised by the Worker. The Owner's own acts are the judgment of the sealed Work against the Intent, the text of `retro-owner.md`, and what is done with an Intent that cannot be delivered as stated, including establishing another in another Change. No actor writes, infers or substitutes them. An actor that is not the Owner does not treat a handoff, a green check or silence as that judgment.

R6. **Continuation after the Owner's act.** After the Owner's judgment and `retro-owner.md` exist, the actor holding the Reviewer role may carry the Change through the remaining legal acts (`retro-review.md`, closing the Change, putting the closed Change forward for host admission) without the Owner acting as a message router. The same holds earlier for the Worker: once a round converges, sealing the Work and writing `retro-work.md` follow without another go.

R7. **Transcription is carrying, not authoring.** An Owner's retrospective supplied by the Owner outside the record may be carried into `retro-owner.md` by another actor verbatim, with nothing added, removed or reworded. That is moving the Owner's text, not writing it.

R8. **Admission stays outside KAAL.** Continuation ends where host mechanics begin: how a closed Change is authorized or admitted is not part of what an actor may carry, and the process says nothing of accounts, approvals or checks.

R9. **Nothing new unless shown necessary.** The Change adds no Node, no artifact type, no status or phase metadata and no role or writer identity, and changes no evaluator, seal, Core or sealed Change. State stays derived from the artifacts. Whether a handoff artifact, a capability or a protocol is needed is a question the Work answers by trying the smallest thing first, and the answer is recorded in the Architecture.

R10. **The Intent is the fixed target.** This Change does not revise its Intent, and its Work does not rewrite sealed history: Change `genesis/26/10/07/02` and every earlier Change stay valid exactly as sealed.

R11. **The test is the Intent's.** After the Owner's one act, can the collaborating agent take the Change from that handoff through the remaining legal process without the Owner becoming a message router? The Agent Skill must make that answer yes, for one actor holding the later roles or several actors, and must say where it is still no.
