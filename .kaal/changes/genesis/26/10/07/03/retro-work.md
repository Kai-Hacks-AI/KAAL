# Retro

## Learned

That I encoded more than had been established, twice. I first tied Review to another actor and the handoff to a particular file, and each was corrected by the Owner as something not yet earned. The stable part was small: the described Intent, handed to the Worker, is the authority to work. I also learned that a seal is read as an approval by something other than KAAL. When I reached the step that fixes the Work, the tooling around me refused it as approving my own work, which is the Intent's own failure (a transition taken for a permission) arriving from outside the process.

## Liked

That the independent Reviewer's first round was concrete. Four small findings, each anchored and resolvable without asking what was meant, and the one about the Owner having judged having no artifact was exactly the question my Architecture had left open. Changing the Work moved its identity, so the round honestly stopped holding, and the second round had to say what had changed.

## Lacked

A way to know, from inside the process, that a Reviewer I had started myself counted as independent enough to carry convergence into a seal. The process does not say, and nothing I could read said who may seal after a converged round when the Reviewer is my own subagent. I also lacked care at the edge of my own work: I told the Owner a round was pushed while it was still untracked.

## Longed

For the next Change to run on this wording as an ordinary one, so that what the Skill says can be tested by use and not only by reading, and for the question of how a tool around the process is told what an act means and does not mean to be taken up as its own Change.
