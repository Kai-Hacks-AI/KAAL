# Retro

## Learned

That a review of this Change needed to distinguish the quality of the Requirements from realization of the Intent. My first chat-only response found no defect in the draft, but it did not record a round or establish that the compass had been delivered. Following ROWING made that distinction concrete: a round names the observed Work and leaves a durable handoff. I also learned that comparing the extracted evaluator with the previous one across existing Changes gives useful evidence for a move whose purpose is availability rather than new behavior.

## Liked

The isolated embedding evidence and the single evaluator made the second review easier to ground. I could inspect the delivered payload, run its command without the repository around it, and compare the old and new process answers. Reading the Worker's account of the layout split alongside the Owner's acceptance of the identity boundary helped me understand what the move exposed without turning those consequences into demands to widen this Change.

## Lacked

A local environment that could run the subprocess-based acceptance tests. The sandbox refused child-process spawning, so I relied on the successful CI run for the full suite and recorded that limit beside the direct checks I could perform. I also lacked a protocol-compliant record at the start because I stopped my initial review in chat; correcting that omission took another exchange and delayed the handoff.

## Longed

For a review environment in which the shipped CLI acceptance tests can run directly, so the reviewer can reproduce the strongest evidence in its own context. I also longed for a review handoff that brings the Change's protocol and current compass answer into view immediately, making the required record as natural to produce as the review itself.
