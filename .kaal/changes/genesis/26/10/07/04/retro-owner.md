# Retro

## Learned

That the deterministic process compass was already substantially present. The important gap was not inventing another process mechanism, but making the existing evaluator useful to an agent: it must say not only where the Change is and what comes next, but whose act comes next. I also learned that deterministic guidance has to be conservative about what the artifacts prove; a wrong instruction is worse than an explicit inability to resolve state.

## Liked

That the Intent caused the Worker to inspect what already existed before building something new. The numbered Requirements emerged without being prescribed and gave Review precise anchors. I also liked that Review broke plausible implementations twice on semantic truth rather than style, and that the Work identity made those iterations unambiguous.

## Lacked

The result is still repository-local. `kaal-changing` itself does not yet carry the deterministic answer wherever KAAL is embedded. That does not prevent this Change from satisfying its stated scope, but it exposes a meaningful boundary between Engineering KAAL and Embedding KAAL.

## Longed

For the same deterministic process compass to become intrinsic to `kaal-changing`, so an agent using KAAL does not need knowledge of this repository's engineering machinery to know where it is, whose act is next, and what that act is.
