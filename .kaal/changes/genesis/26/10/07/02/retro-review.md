# Retro

## Learned

Reading the sealed Work alongside the persisted rounds taught me that the Reviewer’s judgment also needs to remain corrigible. Round 01 asked for symmetric, any-order retrospectives; round 02 explicitly corrected that instruction to ordered WORK. The Worker’s retrospective made the resulting rework visible, while the Owner’s explained why the distinction mattered. In this bootstrap, review was testing a process whose meaning was still being clarified through that same review. The final model alone would have hidden that learning.

## Liked

I could follow the corrections without relying on the final account alone. Each persisted round named the Work it addressed, and the final round named the exact identity whose seal I checked. Reading the Worker and Owner perspectives before writing mine added consequences and purpose to the review record. That sequence helped me reflect on the activity of review without turning this retrospective into another judgment of the Work.

## Lacked

A single, consistent correspondence between the review conversation and the persisted rounds. The WORK design resolution had no round file, so the conversation’s round 04 became `review/03.md`, and the Evidence had to explain the difference. The PR description also retained the initial unsealed handoff. Entering fresh, I had to reconcile those accounts with the artifacts before I could confidently locate my boundary. I can reconstruct the earlier review, but I cannot claim its original writer’s experience as my own.

## Longed

For future review practice to preserve explicit corrections of the Reviewer’s own instructions as readily as it preserves corrections to Work. This record let me see an earlier review instruction being withdrawn rather than silently disappearing. I would like that candour to become ordinary: a Reviewer able to recognize how its judgment shaped the Worker’s effort, learn from the later perspectives, and carry that learning into another Change without rewriting the history of this one.
