# Retro

## Learned

Reviewer independence is an authority and handoff problem, not a model, account or context-counting problem. KAAL's earlier instructions made Worker-created review appear valid, so the failure observed on #65 was systemic. A grant establishes a seat; technical capability alone never does. The process must identify the next seat and stop when it is unoccupied.

## Liked

The correction stays within Changing and ROWING instructions. It preserves authorized orchestration and the human Owner's ability to delegate, while keeping Worker and Reviewer judgments distinct. Review rounds disclosed the authority under which the Reviewer acted, and the independent review found and resolved contradictions before convergence.

## Lacked

Mechanical checks cannot establish the truth of an authority grant or the independence of judgment. That remains an operating responsibility. I also lacked a concise end-to-end view of handoffs across the whole Change lifecycle, although the required Reviewer stop is now clear.

## Longed

For future KAAL instances to make the next seat obvious without prompting an agent to occupy every available role. I want provider adapters to realize explicitly authorized arrangements, while KAAL retains the process meaning and the human retains control of delegation.
