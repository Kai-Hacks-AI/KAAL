# Retro

## Learned

That the Worker's mistake on the earlier Change was ours before it was the Worker's: the installed text called a same-actor Reviewer valid, recommended "separate contexts", and had lost the sentence that once said a helper the Worker starts is not a Reviewer. I also learned that my first framing, a Worker must not appoint its Reviewer, was too crude. The Owner's correction (authority is granted, never inferred from capability) is the actual rule, and it keeps legitimate orchestration. Two of the Reviewer's findings showed how easily I left a contradiction in my own text: I wrote the helper rule once without scope, and left an old "same actor" clause two sentences away from a new rule that forbade it.

## Liked

That the process held me to the Intent. Three rounds corrected the draft before any wording existed, and the Owner's comment turned a rule about a prohibition into a rule about assignment, which is simpler to state and harder to misread. I liked that the Reviewer was visibly a different seat: its rounds said under whose authority it held the role, which is exactly the property this Change asks for.

## Lacked

A way to check text against text. The tests I added assert that certain phrases exist and others do not, which catches a returning phrase but not a contradiction I have not thought of; round 04 found two that my test passed. I also lacked a place to say, in the Intent, which sentences of the old Skill were deliberately being reversed.

## Longed

That the Owner-side stops removed earlier and the Reviewer-seat stop added here could be read together in one short page, so the next Worker sees that "continue" and "stop at an empty seat" are the same rule. And that the evaluator could one day show the Reviewer's stated authority next to its round without judging it.
