# Retro

## Learned

I initially treated a Worker-started review session as categorically disqualified. The Owner's correction made the distinction more precise: authority establishes the seat, and the reviewing actor must exercise judgment apart from the Worker. Starting or resuming a session describes a mechanism; it does not establish the grant or invalidate an existing one. I had to correct my earlier recommendation rather than carry that prohibition into implementation review.

I also learned how easily a process can contradict itself within one document. The new separation rule coexisted with an old same-actor allowance, and an exception for an assigned Reviewer coexisted with an unconditional prohibition on writing rounds. Reviewing the permitted paths, rather than only the presence of the intended phrases, exposed both.

## Liked

The Owner settled the conceptual questions before implementation, and the fixed Intent gave me a stable target for the later rounds. Exact Work identities and preserved rounds made the corrections traceable, including when the whole staged Change moved after another Change was admitted.

The scope stayed small. The review instructions could be corrected without altering sealed meanings, the evaluator or provider adapters. The Worker's retrospective also named the limitations of its wording tests, which matched what I had observed during review.

## Lacked

I lacked a sufficiently precise distinction between authority and session mechanics in my first recommendation. That made the Owner's intervention necessary to preserve legitimate orchestration.

The passing delivery tests provided less assurance than their wording suggested. They checked selected phrases while the two conflicting instructions remained. I needed to assess the complete allowed and refused paths in the delivered instructions to judge what those checks actually supported.

## Longed

I want future instances to make the next seat and its required act easy to see, while keeping the question of who holds that seat explicit and separate. That would support continuation without encouraging a capable actor to fill every role itself.

I also want future instruction reviews to exercise both an unassigned helper and a distinct Reviewer acting under a grant, including their handoffs after findings and convergence. Those concrete paths would help preserve this distinction as the surrounding capabilities evolve.
