# Retro

## Learned

I started by assuming the problem was proof, and spent the first design on what bytes could show. The audit turned it around: nothing in a closed Change can show who wrote what, or even in what order, so the real work was deciding where the process stops and what it asks of the next actor. I also learned that I had been describing a boundary I did not own. Twice I wrote down how an observer comes to occupy its seat, and both times that was machinery outside KAAL; the Change only gets to say what state it is in and what the occupant must be.

## Liked

That the handoff needed no new thing. The evaluator already derives the stage where Work is sealed and only the worker's retro exists, so naming it and telling the worker to stop there was enough, and the evaluator's stages and closure stayed untouched. I also liked having a concrete counterexample to write against; it turned a vague worry about independence into a case the wording could rule out.

## Lacked

A check that tells me my own instructions are KAAL-native. I found the host-flavoured phrasing only because it was pointed out, and had to write a test that scans the wording for selection and orchestration words, which catches the vocabulary and not the idea. I also lacked any way to know, while writing this, whether the observer will read the Skill the way I intend, since I cannot be that reader.

## Longed

For the next step in this process to be something I can only be told, not do: I am the one who wrote the observer's instructions and I am now about to hand them over, and I would like the first real handoff to show whether they are enough on their own, without me adding a word.
