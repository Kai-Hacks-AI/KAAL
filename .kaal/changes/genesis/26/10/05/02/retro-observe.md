# Retro

## Learned

The Work is short enough to hold whole, and its Rule section carries the design in a paragraph: Core owns schema and default, the instance owns the file, the checker only reports. I learned that a sealed identity can be pinned by quoting truncated hashes (`7b247073…`, `ff114bfe…`, `fa393cb5…`), and that "unchanged bytes" is therefore a claim I can test, though not from these two files alone. I also learned the Work leaves two things standing on purpose, the sealed Change `04/01` and the checker's fixture strings, and names both.

## Liked

The Evidence section is written as observations a stranger can repeat: a command, its exit code, a before and an after (two directories red, then all green). I could tell what had been shown and what had not. The Work also states what it declined to do, such as no new public API and no name mapping, which let me read the scope as a decision and not an omission.

## Lacked

The Work gives no path or location for the checker, the fixtures, the installer test or the renamed directories. From these two files I could not see any of them, so the Evidence claims, however clear, could only be taken as written. The hashes are truncated, and nothing in the Work says where the full IDs or seals can be found.

## Longed

To be able to read the sealed Work and be pointed, by reference, at the exact artifacts its Evidence rests on, so that an observer's confidence could come from checking and not from the clarity of the prose.
