# Retro

Retro is the capability for creating a retrospective in its canonical form and for telling whether something is one. A retrospective is one writer's own account of something that writer took part in, in four parts, each a different kind of observation: what the writer Learned, what they Liked, what they Lacked and what they Longed for. Its scope is that alone. It does not decide when a retrospective is owed, who writes it, or what it is about; whatever asks for one decides that. It is not Core, and it is not any one process's retrospective.

Learned is knowledge or understanding the writer gained that was not known or clear at the start. Liked is something that worked well, from the writer's seat, and is worth keeping. Lacked is something absent whose absence made the writer's part harder, weaker, slower or less certain. Longed is a wished-for future possibility or improvement, and is never a restatement of what was Lacked. The four hold different observations: one observation is not written four ways. A retrospective speaks only for its writer, and where the writer has nothing honest to say for a part, it says so plainly.
