## What was delivered
`05-src/lane/qualify.mjs` judges a diff against a table of lane entries `{ kind, roots, name, allow, status }`. A file qualifies only when exactly one entry matches it and its status is listed there (default A and M; add-only kinds say A); a file that fits two entries is refused as `ambiguous-lane`. Collisions are judged per kind on the full resulting tree, the key is capture group 1 of the entry's name pattern, and a finding carries its kind. The roots of an entry must match its `allow` pattern and cover no rule path, else the whole table is refused. The list of existing files is complete evidence per kind or none: a missing or malformed list for a kind the change lands in is UNKNOWN, never qualified. A one-entry item table gives the verdicts and findings of the backlog judge on the inputs of backlog T495 to T499. Cases T1710 to T1715 and T1770 are green.

## What we learned
- **The table is the contract, so the holes are in the table.** Three holds came from what the data could say, not from the judge: an add-only kind needs its own status list, two entries can overlap, and an inventory that is not given must not read as empty. Each is now a Case.
- **Case numbers are shared by name, not by package.** T1716 was taken by controls, so the new Case is T1770. Ids are namespaced, but a number free on every branch is cheaper than an argument.
- **Fail closed on evidence.** "Nothing exists" and "I could not read what exists" are different facts; the second is UNKNOWN. The caller's completeness of the lists stays an integration obligation of the route (C192), where it must be tested with real reads.
- **Core and Backlog adopt it as data.** Core exports its claim row as `idClaimLane(folder)`; Backlog keeps its item entry. Neither needed a change to the judge.
