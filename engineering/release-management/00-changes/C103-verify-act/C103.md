# release/C103 an ACT enters a collector only when the Cases its PLAN added are really green

The Owner's rule of 20 Nov 2026: a merged PLAN on a collector allows the DO, and all real GREEN on the collector allows the ACT. Real GREEN means the PLAN added RED Cases and the DO turned exactly those GREEN: the same Cases, with the inverse expectation. Threads own PDCA and raise their own ACT; the Dispatcher does not raise ACTs and raises a PROMOTION only after an ACT has merged.

This Change is the first of the controls that follow from it: release/CTL24 `verify Act`, a detective control that reads the Cases the merged PLAN added from the base, finds them at the head, and says FAIL, loudly and naming them ("finish your work first"), while any is not really green. Release owns it because release owns the stages (Owner, 20 Nov 2026), until stages or stations get their own home. It is a pure judgement of observations: it never decides from an exit code.

It adds the Intent release/I56, the Requirement release/F80, the Architecture release/A71, the control release/CTL24 and the source `05-src/phase/judge-act.mjs`. Later slices, each its own Change: the workflow and its WIRE, the pre-push hook mirror for early feedback (a WIRE, since `stages.json` is exact), the hand-over of observations from the lanes (engineering's), and a gate in a ruleset (the Owner's, by hand).

## Challenge to the legacy controls

Each new control challenges the legacy ones for retirement. verify Act retires nothing today: no legacy control judges an ACT, so there is nothing it replaces. It does name two things that retire later, in their own Changes and after it runs green on real ACTs: the `.red.mjs` exclusion of the Engineering Case runner and the fail-on-any-RED behaviour of the Workspaces lane (engineering's, once the lanes hand observations to the policy), and the test half of the legacy verify Plan (release/A1), which a later control for PLAN admission (the Cases the PLAN adds observed RED) will challenge.
