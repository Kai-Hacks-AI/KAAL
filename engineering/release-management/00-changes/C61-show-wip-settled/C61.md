# release/C61 Show WIP shows which branches hold settled work

This is WIP cleanup (Lean housekeeping), not an inactivity measure. `kaal/<capability>/<name>`, `hotfix/<name>` and `wire/<name>` are work-item branches: each carries one piece of work, and `kaal/`, `hotfix/` and `wire/` are namespaces, not stock. When that work has reached where it belongs, or was discarded on purpose, the branch has no job left and an authorized person can clean it up. Show WIP (release/C39) already knows the pieces, their destinations and the branches. It now also says, per branch, what became of the work, and has one view of the branches whose work is settled.

The state of a work-item branch:

- **wip**: work is still going: an open piece from the branch, or commits that no settled piece covers.
- **delivered**: the newest piece from the branch merged into its destination and the branch holds nothing beyond that piece's head. A squash promotion leaves the commits unmerged by ancestry, so the proof is the piece's head, not Git ancestry.
- **discarded**: the newest piece from the branch was closed unmerged and the branch holds nothing beyond that piece's head: the work was deliberately dropped or indicated by mistake.
- **UNKNOWN**: the evidence is missing or conflicts. It is never guessed and never settled.

A branch with no commit beyond main and no piece is not "empty" here: nothing proves its work was delivered or dropped (it may be about to start), so it is UNKNOWN.

Settled (delivered or discarded) branches are the cleanup candidates of an authorized person, in a view called `settled`, not `stale`: nothing in it measures age. The view only reports. C61 adds no deletion and no automatic action.
