# release/C103 an ACT enters a collector only when the Cases that apply to its Change are really green

The Owner's rule of 20 Nov 2026: a merged PLAN on a collector allows the DO, and all real GREEN on the collector allows the ACT. A collector is an evolving, artifact-driven workspace, not a reconstruction of its pull request history. It may receive any number of PLANs while the work is underway; each adds or amends Cases together with the artifacts and relationships that justify them, and so changes the outstanding RED obligations. Any number of DOs then turn those Cases GREEN, and exactly one ACT follows. Progress and readiness are measured from the checked-out tree (its files, its relationships, the observed results of its Cases), locally or in CI; pull requests and commits transport and give provenance, and their titles, first-parent history and merge shapes are never how work is found or measured. Threads own PDCA and raise their own ACT; the Dispatcher does not raise ACTs and raises a PROMOTION only after an ACT has merged.

This Change is the first of the controls that follow from it: release/CTL24 `hold Act`, a preventive control: an `act.md` may stand in a Change folder only when the Cases that apply to that Change are really green. Without an `act.md` there is no ACT to hold and it says PASS, whatever is red, so the PLANs and DOs that come before the ACT land (their CI is green while their Cases are really red). With one it finds the Cases that apply to the Change from the content, reads their observed results, and says FAIL, loudly and naming them ("finish your work first"), while any is not really green. Release owns it because release owns the stages (Owner, 20 Nov 2026), until stages or stations get their own home. It is a pure judgement of observations: it never decides from an exit code. Whether a given pull request may change Cases or artifacts is transport authorization, a separate control aligned with kaal-change's ratchet (change/F56); it is not part of this Change.

It was first planned as `verify Act`; the Owner (20 Nov 2026) named the three small controls of the ACT: `hold Act` (this Change), `demand Act` and `verify Act`, and that name now belongs to the check of the ACT's format. It adds the Intent release/I56, the Requirement release/F80, the Architecture release/A71, the control release/CTL24 and the source `05-src/phase/judge-act.mjs`. Later slices, each its own Change: the workflow and its WIRE, the pre-push hook mirror for early feedback, the hand-over of observations from the lanes (engineering's), and the gate in the `23 - collecting` ruleset (the Owner's, by hand). The other two controls of the ACT are their own small Changes (see change.md).

## Challenge to the legacy controls

Each new control challenges the legacy ones for retirement. hold Act retires nothing today: no legacy control judges an ACT, so there is nothing it replaces. It does name two things that retire later, in their own Changes and after it runs green on real ACTs: the `.red.mjs` exclusion of the Engineering Case runner and the fail-on-any-RED behaviour of the Workspaces lane (engineering's, once the lanes hand observations to the policy), and the test half of the legacy verify Plan (release/A1), which a later control for PLAN admission (the Cases the PLAN adds observed RED) will challenge.
