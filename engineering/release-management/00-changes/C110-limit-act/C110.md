# release/C110 an ACT does only ACT work

The fourth small control of the ACT, asked for by the CPO on #2125 and given the go by the Owner (20 Nov 2026): `hold Act` (release/C103) says the work is finished before an ACT enters, `demand Act` (release/C108) asks for it, `verify Act` will check its format, and `limit Act` (this Change) keeps the ACT to ACT activities. The core concept (Owner): limit the ACT to ACT activities, and in general we know who may modify which folder in the PDCA: the folders exist partly so that they can be locked. A hotfix may touch everything and a wire only the delivery folders, each a different route with its own control. `limit Act` is the first row of that write map, kept as data and owned by release.

Today the ACT route writes one folder, the retros: writing a retrospective in `<capability>/02-learnings/retros/` is ACT work. A pull request that changes a retro is an ACT, and it may change nothing outside the folders of that row: rewriting Requirements, Architecture, source or Cases in the same pull request must not pass as reflection. That an ACT pull request is one is read from its content (it touches a folder of the row), never from a label, a title or a branch name.

This Change is the first slice: the Intent release/I59, the Requirement release/F85, the Architecture element release/A75 (the judge), the control release/CTL30 `limit Act` (detective first, preventive only with the Owner's go), the judge `05-src/phase/judge-limit-act.mjs` with its command line, and the write map `05-src/phase/write-map.json` with its first row, the route `act`. It is not the readiness of the collector (release/C103), not the presence of the ACT before promotion (release/C108) and not the format of the retro (`verify Act`).
