# Intent

Intent is the capability of stating what is wanted and why, and of establishing that statement as a target. It primarily serves the Owner, the one whose want it is: it helps them find and say which outcomes they want, why they want them, and within what boundaries, before anyone works out how. A boundary is whatever the outcome must respect: what must not happen, what is not wanted, what is deliberately left open.

Intent stays on that side of the line. It does not define Requirements, Architecture or implementation. Where those arise while an Intent is being described, they are noticed and handed on, not decided inside the Intent; an Intent that has become a design has stopped serving the one who wants. Anyone may help to describe an Intent, but that it is what the Owner wants is the Owner's to say, and nothing records that it was said.

An Intent is a result like any other: whole enough to be examined, by anyone and from any viewpoint, as to whether it says what is wanted and why, whether its boundaries are clear, and whether it has overreached into how. It is identified exactly, by the identity of its bytes, and to establish an Intent is to put it forward as that identified statement and no other. What is changed afterwards is another Intent with another identity, never the established one revised, so that whoever works toward it and whoever judges the outcome measure against one fixed thing. An Intent that cannot be delivered as stated is not edited to fit: what is done with it is the Owner's, and may be another Intent.

Its scope is that alone. Intent prescribes no template for how an Intent is written, and none of its parts is required to have a particular name. It does not decide where Intents are kept, when one is owed, who describes it, who reviews it, how an outcome is judged against it, or what follows. It is not a requirement, not an architecture, not a plan and not a role. It is usable wherever something wanted needs stating, and is bound to no process. Intent is optional: it is not Core, a valid embedding does not need it, and embedding KAAL does not install it.
