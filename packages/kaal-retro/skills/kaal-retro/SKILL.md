# Retro

A retrospective looks back at a subject to understand it and to find what could be better. It works on any subject and needs nothing else installed.

## Subject

The subject may be completed or ongoing work, a decision, an event, an experience, a process, a product or something else. Nothing about the subject has to be finished, formal or tracked.

Before starting, establish with the person asking:

- the **subject**: what is being looked back on;
- the **purpose**: what the retrospective is for;
- the **scope**: the period or boundary, and what is left out;
- the **participants**, where relevant: whose views are needed. If a view is missing, say so. Never write someone's view for them.

If any of these is unclear and cannot be inferred, ask briefly; otherwise state your assumption and proceed.

## Method

Choose a method that fits the subject and the purpose, and say which one you used and why. [4L](references/4l.md) is the first method available. Other methods may be used when they fit better.

## What to keep apart

Label what you write so the reader can tell these apart:

- **Observation**: something that happened or is the case, as far as the evidence shows.
- **Interpretation**: what you or a participant think it means or why it happened.
- **Potential improvement**: something that might be done differently or added.

Every category of a method can yield potential improvements. None is more actionable than another by nature.

## Context and provenance

Keep what a later reader needs: who said it, when, and what it refers to (links, names, dates, documents). Do not invent detail that is not available.

## Size

Size the retrospective to the subject. A small subject deserves a few lines, a large one a short structured page. A retrospective nobody can act on is too long or too vague.

## Decisions

A retrospective creates no Change and decides nothing. It does not approve, authorize, assign or start work. Potential improvements are offered to whoever owns the subject, and they decide whether and how to act on them. State clearly that decisions rest with their owner.
