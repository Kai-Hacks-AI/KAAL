# Leaving a stable job for freelance: 4L retrospective (the decider's viewpoint, one year on)

A 4L retrospective of the decision, written by the person who made it. It looks back and does not close the decision: whether to continue is still open, and nothing here claims to settle it.

## Liked

Income held. I earn about the same as before, so the bet did not cost me the living I had. I also work fewer hours, and on work I choose, which was the reason for leaving. The trade I wanted, control over my time at no loss of pay, is real, not hoped for.

## Learned

Equal pay does not mean equal footing. The same average now comes with more variance, so a year's total hides the months that felt thin. The first four months showed it: with no savings buffer, ordinary gaps between invoices were a threat, not a nuisance, and that likely shaped my choices of work early on more than I admit. I also learned that I cannot judge the decision by the average alone. Hours, choice, variance and loneliness each pull in a different direction, and none cancels another. This is one year of one person's record, so it shows how it went, not whether it would go the same again.

## Lacked

A savings buffer when I started. I left before I had one, and the first four months paid for it. I also lacked colleagues: the daily contact, the people to ask and to be asked, are gone and nothing has yet replaced them. And I lack a defined test for continuing. I have feelings and a rough income figure, but no stated threshold, such as a lowest tolerable month or a runway in months, that would turn "should I continue" into a check.

## Longed For

A runway I can name: a buffer of a stated number of months, held before the next risky step. A standing place for colleague contact, such as a shared workspace, a peer group or a regular call with peers. A short list of conditions under which I would continue and under which I would stop, written now, before the next lean spell colours the answer. A second view, from someone who knows my work, on whether the variance is the market or my own habits. Month-by-month figures for the year, which I would read against these conditions; I have only the average in mind, and I have not looked at them here.
