# retro/F5 lens-homes

The Skill's text gives each of the four lenses its own question and its own home, and the questions differ in what they ask about, not in tense:

- **Liked** asks what I relied on that held up and should be kept. It is about something that exists.
- **Learned** asks what I found when I went looking outside the consumer. It is about a source.
- **Lacked** asks what was missing when I had to decide, and what that cost me. It is about an observed blocker.
- **Longed For** asks what I would like to exist that I never needed. It is about something that did not block me.

The text also says what to avoid, in a short paragraph. An **echo** is the same point I say again: a Lacked that reappears as a Longed For, a Learned that repeats the fix a Lacked already names, or one entry I write twice in different words. A **mirror** is the same thing seen from both sides: a Liked X and a Lacked absence of X, such as "the finder exists" and "nothing points to the finder". Both are avoided by one rule: a point has one home. Where it cost me something it is a Lacked, and otherwise it sits where it exists or where I found it: something that helped and cost nothing stays in Liked. A Learned says what the outside source says and never what to do here, because what to do belongs to the Lacked it bears on. The boundary between Lacked and Longed For is guarded in both directions with one test, what I did differently because of the absence. A real blocker must not be filed as a wish: if its absence made me take a wrong step, a detour, a correction or a question I should not have needed, it is a Lacked even though I coped in the end. A wish must not be inflated into a blocker: a cost that only could have happened, or would have been nicer to avoid, is a Longed For, and a Lacked names a cost that did happen. If I changed nothing, it is a Longed For and its `worked-anyway` line is true. The paragraph says that a form check sees a repeated subject but not an echo or a mirror under different words, so I decide while filling the lenses, and there is no checklist of questions. The text is written in the first person throughout, as the agent's own words ("what did I rely on"), and asks for no new field, script or section.
