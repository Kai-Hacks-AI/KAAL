# Portability Across Capabilities: Intent

This is `hosting/C16`, a PLAN Change. It extends the platform coupling conformance suite of `hosting/C13` (#650) so that Hosting, as the one operating-system boundary, decides every other capability's portability: which package is the boundary, which host facts a case may depend on, how each is established on a host, the fixtures that need them, the rules for applicability, the review guide per host, and an audit of every capability's applicability declarations. It ships no code. `hosting/C17` delivers it and turns the planned tests GREEN. It authorizes no DO, sync, promotion or publication: those stay with the Owner.

On #577 (comment 6535374084, 2026-10-30) the Owner gave the direction: every KAAL capability tests its own behaviour, and Hosting tests every capability's portability. Hosting is the only capability allowed to own operating-system-specific implementation and test mechanisms. Guard consumes Hosting's cross-platform suite, fixtures, applicability rules and review guides, and does not keep its own platform governance (`engineering/guard/acceptance/platforms.mjs`, guard/T132). guard/T114 stays a Guard behavioural Test Case, but its FIFO and Unix socket fixture, and the evidence and decision that it is genuinely not applicable on Windows, come from Hosting's contract. Engineering runs the gates and records evidence; Testing owns general test-result semantics. Neither takes over Hosting's operating-system policy. On #668 (comment 6535329511) the Owner said the same of Testing: Hosting's suite and review guides challenge every capability's implementation, assertions, test selection and applicability.

Two KAAL contracts meet here. Testing's applicability (testing/F47) lets a case be not applicable by `platforms`, `requires` or `absent`, and testing/F67 (#668) makes a `platforms` scope an architectural violation in every package that is not the operating-system boundary, given as `osBoundary: true`. Which package is the boundary is Hosting's rule. Guard asked, on #671 (guard/C118 revision 3), for three things from Hosting: a special-entry fixture, or the host fact and evidence that a host cannot hold one; applicability rules and a review guide per host, with evidence; and a portability audit over every capability's test files.

- **hosting/I10.** No capability other than Hosting names an operating system, in its code, its tests or its applicability. Where a case genuinely needs something only some hosts have, it names a host fact that Hosting defines. Hosting establishes on each host, by trying, whether that fact holds, gives the fixture, and states the evidence per host in a guide a reviewer can check. Hosting's audit finds every applicability scoped by platform outside the boundary, every host exception that names an unknown fact or lacks a reason, evidence or a registered case, and every case a platform decision selects or weakens. It puts every host exception to Review. So a case is either the same on every host or visibly not applicable for a reason Hosting established, never silently omitted or weakened.
