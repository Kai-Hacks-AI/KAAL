# Collecting KAAL

Collecting KAAL is the capability for bringing communication addressed to KAAL into KAAL from the KAAL clients it can reach. A client is another installation of KAAL; it may carry communication intended for KAAL, and what it carries for KAAL is its own decision. Collecting is the KAAL side of that: it takes what a client exposes, when some available means of reaching the client permits it, and keeps it with the identity of the client it came from and of each carrier. Its scope is that alone. It is not the carriers, it does not interpret, rank or act on what it collects, and it is not any means of reaching a client. It is not Core.

What a client exposes is only the KAAL-addressed carriers it carries within its embedded KAAL. Collecting has no claim on anything else of the client, and reaching a client is not permission to read more than that. How the exposure comes to be seen, whether through a checkout, a host or an archive, belongs to whatever means the collecting agent has, and no such means is part of what a client or a carrier is.

Three things are kept apart and are never one set. A client may be known, because someone can name it. A client may be reachable, which is a fact about one attempt in one environment, not about the client: it may be reached by one agent and not another, or by one means and not another. And clients known and clients reachable are not all the clients there are. A collection therefore speaks only of the attempts it made, each ending reached or unreached, and no collection states, or lets a reader infer, that a client it did not attempt is absent. Collecting keeps no list of clients, so that what is visible is never mistaken for what exists.

A client is named in a collection by its collector's own name for it, which is a name and not where it was found. A carrier is identified by the SHA-256 of its exact bytes and is kept exactly as carried, under the name of the client it came from. How a client was reached is evidence, recorded beside what was collected, and is not identity. What was collected is evidence only: turning it into a request, a decision or a change is another concern, and a collection is written once and never altered.
