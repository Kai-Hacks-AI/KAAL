# hosting/C210 Hosting says what kind of failure a platform call was, in one type

Today a failed platform call tells its caller very little. Hosting's own errors keep GitHub's status and headers on the write calls (C117, C174) but not on the reads, and they name no kind: a rate limit, a refusal, a missing object, a lost connection and a switched-off setting all look alike unless the caller reads headers and text itself. A check that wraps a call can then hide the real answer: the workflow token's rate limit was swallowed by a check, and GitHub's Dependency Review told a pull request that "Dependency graph" is not enabled when the real cause was the installation's API limit (the re-run passed with no setting changed). Controls cannot carry the real cause into its verdicts (controls/C179), and Release's interim raw diagnostics (release/C200) have to print what they cannot classify.

Hosting owns platform mechanics and the one standard every App and every workflow-token read goes through (see the App standard in `03-architecture/architecture.md`). This Change adds the one typed failure to that standard: a closed list of kinds, what the kind rests on, what was asked and where, GitHub's own words with credentials taken out, and the limit figures when it was a limit.

Hosting decides nothing about what a failure means for a verdict: Controls maps a kind to UNKNOWN and writes the guidance (controls/C179), Release retires its interim diagnostics (release/C200) when it adopts this. Hosting only says what it was.

Provisional ids: C210, hosting/F115, A52, and T360 to T363 (serving hosting/I22). They are checked against `main` and every remote branch: the C118 collector holds I25, F113, A50 and T349 to T353, the C174 collector F114, A51 and T356 to T359. Release's C200 to C202, Controls' C179 to C181 and Core's C250 and C251 are not Hosting's.
