# Pull Promotion: Requirements (target)

Scoped `pull/`. F are functional, N nonfunctional. The control is `pull Promotion`, a detective control: it states whether one collector is eligible for promotion and authorizes nothing. It uses Analyze Flow's words (piece, into, from, OPEN, REVIEW, REVISE, LAND, WAIT) and adds only the verdicts READY, BLOCKED and NOT JUDGED and the judgment statuses PASS, FAIL and UNKNOWN.

- **pull/F1. One script in the Skill, `pull Promotion`.** `skills/kaal-release/scripts/pull-promotion.mjs` exports `pullPromotion(facts, declaration, flow, collector)` and prints the same from a command line (`node pull-promotion.mjs <facts.json> <declaration.json> <flow.json> <collector>`). `SKILL.md` names it and the package carries it. It is read-only: it reads three files and prints. It is a separate file from `analyze-flow.mjs`, which it never imports: the two meet in data only (decision in analysis.md, section 4).
- **pull/F2. Inputs.** `facts` and `flow` are the data of Analyze Flow (`coordination/F2`, `coordination/F4`) as they are, for the whole repository. `declaration` is `coordination/F3` plus one section, `readiness`: `owners` (the consumer's word for the owner of each judgment), `lifecycle` (the piece types a Change must have landed in the collector; for KAAL PLAN, DO and ACT, with the Check being the checks), `dependencies` (explicit, `{ change, on: [{ change, collector }] }`), `legacy` (`{ change, reason }`, the Changes that predate the one-collector rule), `checkOwners` (which judgment each named check belongs to). `collector` names the one collector to judge; the facts must hold it.
- **pull/F3. No competing state model.** The Script reads only the fields `coordination/F2` and `coordination/F4` define, plus four optional additions it requests from Analyze Flow's supplier: `piece.landedAt` and `delivery.landedAt` (when it landed), `collector.checks` (the checks on the collector's head, for a collector with no promotion piece yet), and one output field of Analyze Flow, `lane.active` (the collector of the active pair, `coordination/F8` made explicit), and `gap.affects` (which of the six judgments a gap bears on, `pull/F19`). An absent addition is an unknown fact, never defaulted.
- **pull/F4. The verdict.** Six judgments, in this order: `lifecycle`, `verification`, `protection`, `sequencing`, `validation`, `attribution`. Each has an `owner` (declared), a `status` (PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN), a `because` for every status but PASS, the `evidence` that caused it and the `missing` evidence. The verdict is BLOCKED when any judgment FAILs, else NOT JUDGED when any is UNKNOWN, else READY. UNKNOWN never passes and a FAIL is never softened by another judgment.
- **pull/F5. lifecycle (owner: the Change).** A Change is scoped to one collector and is driven there through its own PLAN, DO, Check and ACT (Owner decision, 12 Nov 2026); the ACT must have landed in that collector before the Change is eligible for promotion. The Changes in a collector are those named by the pieces that landed into it since its last delivery (`coordination/F13`; all of them when it has none) or that are still open into it. Each must have a landed piece of every declared `lifecycle` type **in this collector**: a piece in another collector or on the target is never counted, and a Change cannot borrow the completion of another. PASS when every Change is complete; FAIL naming each Change and the missing types, or the open pieces of a Change not landed yet; UNKNOWN when no lifecycle is declared, when no Change is in the collector, or when a piece cannot be placed against the last delivery because a landing time is absent (a piece that cannot be placed makes the missing types UNKNOWN, not FAIL). There is no partial promotion: no declaration of what a promotion "carries" makes an incomplete Change promotable.
- **pull/F6. Changes and the legacy.** The result lists each Change in the collector (`changes`): `complete`, or `incomplete` with the missing types. A Change that a `legacy` entry names is flagged `legacy` with its reason: it predates the rule that work spanning collectors is decomposed into separate Changes. The flag identifies and never excuses: a legacy Change is judged by F5 like any other and is never treated as compliant retroactively.
- **pull/F7. Checks are judged by their owner.** Each check named in `checkOwners` belongs to one judgment (for KAAL: Testing's `verification`, Guard's `protection`, Release's `sequencing`, the Change's `lifecycle`; others as declared). Evidence is the checks on the collector's open promotion piece, else the collector's own checks, else none. A declared check that is absent, running or pending is UNKNOWN and named; a failure is FAIL naming the check and traced to the declared owner; a check reported twice with different conclusions is UNKNOWN; a check that no owner is declared for is not judged: it is carried into `notJudged` and makes `attribution` UNKNOWN. The Script reads conclusions only, never logs or prose.
- **pull/F8. sequencing (owner: Release).** FAIL when the collector is behind the target, UNKNOWN when the distance is not in the facts, plus the checks of F7 declared to it. Relationships between Changes are explicit dependencies, not phases of one Change spread across collectors (Owner, 12 Nov 2026): for each Change in the collector, each `dependencies` entry names the Change and the collector it depends on, whose current version must already be delivered (`coordination/F13`). One that is not delivered is FAIL naming the Change it waits for; an absent collector or delivery fact is UNKNOWN. A wiring follows the completed, delivered capability and borrows neither its ACT nor its completion evidence; that is F5. This prevents cheating: a wiring that lands first lets the contract be weakened until it goes green. Whose turn it is comes from `lane.active`: `active`, `waiting` (naming the active collector) or `not judged`. The turn never changes the verdict: eligibility and turn are separate statements.
- **pull/F9. validation (owner: Review).** Each landed piece of each Change in the collector must have been approved at the version it landed at. UNKNOWN when its review fact is absent or null, FAIL when it was approved at another version or changes were requested. The review of the promotion piece itself stays Analyze Flow's REVIEW finding.
- **pull/F10. Recommendation.** One `next` action in Analyze Flow's five words, executed by no one: a declared hold on the collector or its promotion piece gives WAIT with status `escalate` for the owner, whatever the verdict and whatever Analyze Flow found: a hold never yields OPEN, REVIEW, REVISE or LAND; READY with a promotion piece gives Analyze Flow's own finding for that piece (`source: flow`), never a second OPEN; READY without a piece, on the lane's turn, gives OPEN a PROMOTION for the author; READY off its turn gives WAIT naming the active collector; BLOCKED gives REVISE for the author naming each failing owner and cause; NOT JUDGED gives no action, only the `missing` prerequisites by owner. The text holds no command, no approval and no bypass.
- **pull/F11. Deterministic.** The same inputs give byte-identical output, whatever the order of the pieces, the checks and the lists. The output holds no time.
- **pull/F12. Readiness is not authorization.** The output has no approval, landing or authorization field. Authorities are only the declared roles; with none declared none is invented and the gap is reported. At most one collector's `next` is ready at once, because only the lane's collector can be OPEN; every other READY collector WAITs. The Owner's go on a promotion, the merge queue and the rulesets are untouched.
- **pull/F13. Runs where Node runs.** The command reads its files and nothing else (no environment, no network, no host credential), exits 0 whenever it printed a verdict (BLOCKED and NOT JUDGED are answers) and non-zero only when an input cannot be read or the collector is not in the facts, naming which.
- **pull/F19. A gap that bears on a judgment is not a note.** `facts.gaps` and `flow.notJudged` list what the supplier or the flow could not read. Analyze Flow emits each as prose (a string); a structured gap `{ subject, missing, affects? }` is read as well; anything else is malformed. The Script decides for each whether it bears on one of the six judgments, in this order and by no other rule: (1) `gap.affects`, when given, names the judgment; a name that is not one of the six makes `attribution` UNKNOWN; (2) the consumer's declaration, `readiness.gapRules` (`{ match, affects, reason }`, `match` a pattern over the gap's text, `affects` a judgment or null for a limitation of the model), is the one place that says a gap is unrelated; (3) a structured gap whose subject is the collector bears on `sequencing`, a check with a declared owner on that owner's judgment, a piece of a Change judged here (or going into or coming from the collector) on `lifecycle`, and any other subject on none; (4) a prose gap bears on every judgment it names: the collector (`sequencing`), a check with a declared owner (that owner), a piece or a Change judged here (`lifecycle`); a prose gap that names only things outside the collector bears on none; (5) a prose or malformed gap that names nothing known and that no rule classifies cannot have its relevance established, so it fails safe and makes `attribution` UNKNOWN. A gap that bears on a judgment makes it UNKNOWN, so the verdict is never READY and no action to proceed is recommended. A gap that bears on none stays in `notJudged` with `blocking: false` and `affects: null`. Each gap appears once, in the order given, as the original text (or object) with `blocking` and `affects`. UNKNOWN never overrides FAIL.
- **pull/N1. Standalone.** No runtime dependency; the Script imports nothing but built-ins and not `analyze-flow.mjs`.
- **pull/N2. Generic.** The Script and its fixtures contain no KAAL-specific identity and no host term (`release/F5`); the KAAL declaration lives in the Delivery.
- **pull/N3. Honest.** The README states that the Script judges eligibility on the evidence it is given, recommends only, and verifies and authorizes nothing.
