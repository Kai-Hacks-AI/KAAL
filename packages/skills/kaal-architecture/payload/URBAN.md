# URBAN

Use, Rent, Buy, Adapt, New.

URBAN is an **Enterprise Architecture principle** for technical decisions about making required capability available for use.

- **Use** — the required capability is already available for use.
- **Rent** — an existing market capability is made available as a service operated by somebody else's engine (XaaS).
- **Buy** — an existing market capability is acquired so our own engine operates/hosts it.
- **Adapt** — change something already available through Use, Rent or Buy so the required capability is available for use.
- **New** — build what is missing so the required capability becomes available for use.

Rent and Buy both carry cost; price is not their distinction. Rent accepts the service cost profile of somebody else's engine. Buy puts the operational cost profile on our engine — including compute, maintenance, Human/Agent effort, complexity and opportunity cost even where no useful monetary number exists. A free externally operated service can still be Rent; free/open-source self-hosted software can still be Buy.

URBAN is decision-relative and compositional, not a permanent artifact label. Capability previously made available through Rent or Buy may later simply be Used. U/R/B concern how existing capability becomes available; authored Engineering implementation contains **Adapt, New, or both**, while potentially also Using, Renting or Buying capability.

The name carries two bridges. URBAN extends classic **Make-or-Buy**, and deliberately invokes **urban/city planning**: Enterprise Architecture is analogous to city planning rather than being the builder of every building. These are Human cognitive bridges, not decorative wordplay. They do not import further Make-or-Buy doctrine or properties of real city planners.

When URBAN requires an External absence claim — for example that no adequate Rent or Buy option exists — model prior knowledge is not External research. Support the absence claim at the width needed, or leave it open. Reuse an existing representation before inventing another one, but do not let a Use decision hide Adapt/New authored in the implementation.
