# Consumer Verifier Conformance: Intent

This is `testing/C29`, a PLAN Change. It ships no code. `testing/C30` turns its planned tests GREEN.

**What the Owner set.** On #629 (review 5704237140), the Owner asked that independent consumer verifiers be tested against one shared observable result contract, covering zero or absent tests, a tampered or missing manifest, skipped or inapplicable cases, failures, and the exact installed-package evidence, and that matching result labels never count as proof of equivalent semantics. The Owner's clarification there (comment 6507310201) places it:
- Testing owns the result and evidence semantics and the conformance cases.
- Packaging owns the package entrypoint convention.
- Each package owns its verifier and its tests, and duplicated verifier code is accepted.
- Engineering checks each installed package's verifier against Testing's conformance cases in its own Change, requested on #625.

Reviewing this PLAN on #639 (review 5705708256), the Owner required applicability to be explicit and independently established: a declared platform scope and a reason, checked by Engineering's harness. An ordinary skip or todo, or a verifier's unsupported claim that a case is inapplicable, must never become verified. Package verification stays distinct from a consumer's Change delivery.

The Owner then clarified (comment 6509984871) that `not applicable` is chiefly an anti-cheating and accountability outcome, not a convenient platform escape hatch. It guards against two evasion modes: silent omission of a declared case on an inconvenient platform, and conditional weakening, where a registered case branches on the platform to weaker or no meaningful assertions while reporting GREEN. Every declared case stays visible in evidence. Not applicable is explicit, independently justified against declared applicability, and never counted as passed. Equivalent case identity and intended assertion strength across supported platforms are the default, and Hosting is the domain-specific exception. Engineering's #641 owns execution and static enforcement.

The Owner also set (comment 6517587612) that Testing owns the general diagnostic and reporting contract, while each Test Case owner implements the specific guidance. A run that is not successful or is unresolved reports the observation, the established cause and evidence if known, the uncertainty if any, and a next step: precise (fix a prerequisite and retry), diagnostic (inspect evidence), or exploratory (`analyze`) where no justified remedy is known. No precise fix is fabricated, and no automated recovery is required. Testing does not own any capability's remediation.

Re-reviewing against Packaging's baseline (review 5717423802), the Owner asked for two bounded clarifications. First, applicability for an absent optional complement, under the composition rule, must be explicit and independently established. It must be distinct from a missing required dependency, and must never yield a false verified. Second, Testing must decide, explicitly and grounded in what was established, whether a tampered installation is unknown or failed. Graph's #627 says failed, and Guard and Hosting say cannot verify. testing/F47 now covers optional complements, and testing/F50 records the decision with a follow-up map for each product.

**What exists.** testing/F11–testing/F17 (`testing/C9`) state consumer verification: one entry point that fails closed, exactly one result of verified, failed or unknown, each with its own exit status and summary, and evidence naming what was exercised. testing/A9 records npm as the first realization, with the exit statuses 0, 1 and 2 and one machine-readable summary line. Guard ships its own verifier, Hosting plans one (hosting/F20–hosting/F26 on #629), and Graph another (#627). Each prints its own summary line and uses its own word for unknown, such as Guard's "cannot verify".

- **testing/I14**: Every consumer verifier is held to the same observable behaviour, whoever implements it. Testing states that behaviour as conformance cases: the change a publisher's harness makes in a fresh consumer, and what any conforming verifier must then show. A verifier conforms only when everything it shows agrees with what the harness itself established: its exit status, its result, its cause, each case's outcome, and the installed package's evidence. A matching result word alone never conforms. Testing judges observations and runs nothing: packing, installing, changing the consumer and running the verifier are the harness's, in Engineering's Change.
