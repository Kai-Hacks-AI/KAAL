# Good: every capability says what good looks like

## Principle

Quality is defined by the capability that owns the work, not by KAAL. KAAL only asks that each capability say what good means for its own artifacts, in a form others can read and challenge. KAAL defines no shared measure and does not compare capabilities. Agents are never given a "Good" answer as a target to optimise; the CPO and the capability read it.

## The four questions

Each capability answers, about its own work:

1. **Good.** What does a good outcome look like to its user?
2. **Bad.** What does bad look like? Name concrete failures.
3. **Notice.** How would we notice, from the capability's own artifacts: a Case, a check, a review question, or the judgement of a named person? Where only judgement is possible, say so.
4. **Wrong.** What would show that this definition of good is wrong?

An honest answer may be "we cannot tell yet". The questions are fixed; the answers belong to the capability.

## Where the answers live

In the capability's own Requirements, as a chapter called `Good`, beside its non-functional requirements. Each statement of what good is carries the Cases or review questions that observe it. No new file type, template field, check or ruleset.

## How KAAL learns

- The ACT 4L gets one line: did our Good notice the problem we actually had?
- The CPO reads those lines. A definition that missed a real problem is revised by its capability. A definition that proved useful may be copied by another capability, never imposed.
- No measure is promoted to KAAL-wide. A measure appears centrally only if several capabilities independently adopted it and the CPO decides it should be shared.

## Not in this plan

Rolling out to any capability, choosing a pilot, and any metric. Improvement versus before KAAL cannot be shown from this repository and needs an external baseline from the Owner or a matched with/without trial.
