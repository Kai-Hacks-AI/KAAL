# Good: what a good Change looks like

## Principle

Every capability owns its own Good. This reference states Change's own and nothing else. It is not a method for other capabilities, defines no shared measure and does not compare capabilities. Review can review anything, and owns no other capability's Good.

## The four questions, answered for a Change

The four questions are Change's way of stating its own Good. Another capability may ask the same questions or different ones.

1. **Good.** A Change delivers the Intent and Requirements it states onto its one architecture component. Nothing outside that scope changes. Its record lets a reader see what was replaced, what stayed protected and what shows the target was reached.
2. **Bad.** The delivered scope differs from the stated scope. A Change affects something outside its boundary without naming it. The target is claimed without evidence. Two Changes claim the same number. The record contradicts what `main` holds.
3. **Notice.** The Change's Cases are green at the exact head. The isolation findings show no unaccounted crossing of the boundary. The transition evidence shows the target was reached. The ACT 4L has one line: did our Good notice the problem we actually had? Whether the scope was the smallest sensible one is a judgement of the CPO, and is stated as such.
4. **Wrong.** The definition is wrong if Changes that meet all of the above still need follow-up Changes for the same Intent, or if defects found after landing sit inside the stated scope.

An honest answer may be "we cannot tell yet".

## Where the answers live

In this reference, owned by `kaal-change`. Each statement of what good is carries the Cases or review questions that observe it.

## Not in this plan

A general method for defining Good, a home for such a method, any claim about another capability's Good, a rollout, a pilot and any metric.
