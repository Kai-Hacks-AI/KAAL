# change/C15 succession

Kai asked on 26 November 2026 for an edge between Change records: the successor knows its predecessors, and only for hard dependencies. Change already defines the relationship (`capability/Succeeds.md`, change/I2, change/F4, change/F12) and reads it from Nodes (`lib/delivery.mjs`). It does not define how a Change Record states it, which dependencies are worth declaring, or what a reader of records must report. This PLAN does that. Change owns the format and, on Kai's decision of 26 November, the two detective controls that read it, `change/check Predecessor` and `change/identify Conflict`; Release runs them at PROMOTION. The controls are a sibling PLAN (change/C16) after this one, so this PLAN stays the format. It starts no implementation, adds no control and installs nothing.

## Baseline

`Succeeds` is an Edge from a Change Node to the Nodes it succeeds, pinned to their exact bytes, with a finding for an unresolved or stale target and for a cycle. A repository's Change Record (`C<n>.md` in a Change folder) has no field that states those Edges, so no record says what it depends on and no consumer can read it. Nothing says that only a hard dependency is declared, and nothing keeps file overlap out of the statement: Release's own set of paths a collector owns (release CTL33) is where overlap is known, and it must stay there.

## PLAN

Adds I32, F61 to F64, N18 and the Cases T1557 to T1562 and T1570, parked RED (`.red.mjs`). `fixtures.mjs` is shared by the Cases and is not one. The format, the rules and the interface below are the target; the DO delivers them unchanged.

- **F61. Statement.** A Change Record may hold `succeeds`, a list of entries `{ change, pin, because }`, in its frontmatter. `change` is the predecessor's identity (`<namespace>/C<n>`, change/F2). `pin` is the SHA-256, lowercase hexadecimal, of the exact bytes of the predecessor's record file, which is Core's content address (a name or location is never part of it). The reader takes the derivation as an argument, `pinOf(text)`; a consumer passes `sha256` of `@kaihacksai/kaal-foundation`, and the Cases inject the same function. `because` is one line saying what fails without the predecessor on main. A Change that succeeds nothing states nothing.
- **F62. Hard dependencies only.** An entry with no `because`, or an empty one, is a finding `not-hard` naming the Change and the predecessor. Whether the reason is true is a reviewer's to judge; the format only requires that someone gave one. A malformed entry (an identity that is not `<namespace>/C<n>`, a pin that is not 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, a missing field) is `malformed`, and a repeated predecessor is `duplicate`.
- **F63. Validity.** Against the records given: a predecessor that is not present is `unresolved`; one present whose bytes no longer give the pin is `stale`, naming the pinned and the present identity, reported and never repaired, and it is not an Edge; a cycle, and a Change that succeeds itself, are `cycle` naming each Change, sorted (change/F12). The valid Edges are returned with their `because`. These are the same findings as change/F4 and change/F12, read from records instead of Nodes.
- **F64. Only the successor states it; nothing derived is written.** What succeeds a Change is derived from the other records' entries (`successors(identity)`), never stated. A record that holds `successors`, `succeededBy` or `precedes` is `derived-stated`. File overlap is never declared: a record that holds `overlaps`, `conflicts` or `files` is `overlap-stated`; overlap is derived from the paths each collector owns, which is Release's.
- **N18. Pure and host-free.** The reader is `readSuccession(records, { pinOf })` in `lib/succession.mjs`, over `[{ identity, text }]` and the derivation it is handed. It reads no file, no process and no network and names no host. Which records to read, and from where, is the caller's: Release reads files on a branch, another consumer reads another source.

Defaults to leave open: the exact finding names are the Cases', and a consumer may add its own.

## Identity, satisfaction and revision

The CPO asked that three things stay apart, and that nothing here redefine Succeeds. They are three different facts about an edge, each with its own proof:

| Fact | What it says | Where it lives | Established by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dependency identity** | which Change the successor depends on | `change`, the predecessor's identity `<namespace>/C<n>` (change/F2) | the successor's record; never changes with an edit |
| **Dependency satisfaction** | whether the predecessor is delivered, so the dependency holds | not in the record, not in the pin | change/I10 for a Change (its required Cases GREEN and its own predecessors delivered), and, before a promotion, `change/check Predecessor` (change/C16): the predecessor is on main, or carried with the successor |
| **Revision binding** | which version of the predecessor the successor was written against | `pin`, the SHA-256 of the exact bytes of the predecessor's record file, as Core defines a Node's id | the bytes themselves, compared by the reader |

- **What is pinned.** Only the predecessor's record file `C<n>.md`, exactly as Succeeds already says: an Edge pins A's exact bytes. Not the rest of A's folder, not its code, not its Cases.
- **Why bytes, and why the record.** A hard dependency is a dependence on what A promised, its scope, its requirements and its Cases, and that is what its record states. If those bytes change, the promise the successor relied on may have changed, so the successor's author must look again. The pin is the means to notice that. This is the existing Definition (`capability/Succeeds.md`, change/F4: a target changed after it was pinned is `stale`), read from a record instead of a Node. This PLAN adds a place to write the edge and rules about reading it; it does not change what an Edge is or what stale means.
- **A pin is not delivery.** A matching pin says the successor was written against these bytes. It says nothing about whether the capability is delivered: unchanged bytes can be on a branch that never merged. The reader therefore reports edges and findings and never a word of delivery (T1570); satisfaction is judged elsewhere, as the table says.
- **Delivery is not a pin either.** A predecessor delivered under other bytes is still `stale`: the dependency is satisfied and the revision binding is broken. Both facts are reported, neither stands for the other.
- **What an amendment does to successors.** Editing a predecessor's record, even a correction of wording, makes every successor that pins it `stale` until its author states the dependency again with the new pin. That cost is deliberate and is the Definition's: the pin is reported, never repaired, and never moved by a tool. It is accepted because only hard dependencies are declared, so the successors of a record are few, and re-pinning is one line the author makes after checking that the reason in `because` still holds. A PLAN that amends a Change with successors must say so, and may name them with the derived `successors` view.
- **Left out on purpose.** Pinning identity only (no revision) would never notice a changed promise. Pinning a slice of the record (its requirements, say) would need a definition of the slice that Change does not own. Neither is proposed; if the cost above proves too high in use, relaxing the binding is a separate PLAN against the Definition, not a quiet change of reading.

## What uses it

The sibling PLAN change/C16 defines `change/check Predecessor` (a declared hard predecessor is on main at its pin) and `change/identify Conflict` (overlap of owned paths between open collectors), both reading records through `readSuccession`. Release runs them at PROMOTION and decides nothing else about them. This Change defines no control, check, flag or ruleset.

## Questions

1. **Pin derivation, settled by Core.** A Node's id is the SHA-256 of its exact bytes, lowercase hex. Core exports no function for it from its index; `sha256(bytes)` in `@kaihacksai/kaal-foundation` computes it for any bytes, a plain record file included. `identify` is not used: it throws unless the bytes are a Node. A content-address helper exported by Core would be a separate Core Change.
2. **Stale after an amendment.** Settled in the section above: it is the Definition, accepted for hard dependencies only.
3. **Case ids.** T1557 to T1562, T1570 and I32, F61 to F64, N18, G18, C15 were checked against `main` and every remote branch on 26 November 2026. Release confirms hand-picked ids.
