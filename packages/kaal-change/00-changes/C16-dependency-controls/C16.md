# change/C16 dependency controls

Kai decided on 26 November 2026 that Change owns both controls over the dependencies between Changes, under its own namespace: `change/check Predecessor` and `change/identify Conflict`. The controls are framed over Changes and the paths they claim, with no word of collectors or Git. Release maps its collectors to Changes when it calls them, runs them at PROMOTION and in its own checks, and decides nothing else about them. This PLAN defines the two judgements and declares the controls. They are detective: they reveal, they gate nothing. It starts no implementation, adds no workflow, flag, ruleset or WIRE, and installs nothing.

It builds on change/C15, which defines how a record states the Changes it succeeds and the reader `readSuccession`. Release's held PLAN (release/C161, #2708) drafted the two judgements, their facts and their acceptance expectations; this PLAN takes them over as Change's, with Release's agreement. It maps Release's behaviour Cases to T1563 to T1566 and writes its own structure Case for its entries (T1569), since Release's asserted Release paths.

## Baseline

A Change can state a predecessor (change/C15), and the paths a collector owns are known to Release's control `isolate Changes` (release/CTL33). Nothing checks, before a PROMOTION, that a declared hard predecessor is delivered, and nothing tells that two open Changes claim the same path or whether a declared edge orders them. That knowledge lives in project memory and PR text.

## PLAN

Adds I33, F65 to F67, N19 and the Cases T1563 to T1569, parked RED (`.red.mjs`; `fixtures.mjs` is shared and is not a Case), and the two control records at `14-operations/controls/CTL1-check-predecessor/CTL1.md` and `CTL2-identify-conflict/CTL2.md`, as the DO adds them. The drafts of the records are in `controls.md` of this folder.

- **F65. check Predecessor** (`change/CTL1`). `checkPredecessor({ pinOf, main, carried, elsewhere, delivered })` judges the Changes under check over records given as `[{ identity, text }]`: `main` are the records main holds, `carried` those of the Changes under check (the same promotion), `elsewhere` those of other Changes known but not delivered, and `delivered` the identities that Change's own delivery judgement (change/I10, `assess` over the evidence its caller selected) reports delivered. It reads each carried Change's `succeeds` through `readSuccession` over `main` and `carried` together. A predecessor counts only when the dependency is **satisfied**, which is not the same as its record bytes matching the pin: it is satisfied by being on main, or by being carried in the same promotion **and** delivered (its own required Cases GREEN and its own predecessors delivered). PASS when every declared predecessor is satisfied, and when no Change declares one (the note says that is no declared prerequisite, not a claim that none exists). FAIL, one finding per Change and predecessor, for `unresolved` (found nowhere), `stale` (present with other bytes), `elsewhere` (only a Change elsewhere holds it), `undelivered` (carried with matching bytes but not delivered), and for each format finding of the reader (`not-hard`, `malformed`, `duplicate`, `cycle`, `derived-stated`, `overlap-stated`). UNKNOWN, naming what it cannot read, when `pinOf`, `main`, `carried` or `delivered` are missing or malformed: never a PASS.
- **F66. identify Conflict** (`change/CTL2`). `identifyConflict({ pinOf, changes, resolved })` takes `changes: [{ identity, text, claimed }]`: the record of each open Change and `claimed`, the paths it claims that main does not hold (Release derives them from the `own` set of release/CTL33 when it maps a collector to its Changes), and optionally `resolved: [{ path, before, after }]`, the shared paths that synchronization has resolved (Release establishes it when the later collector holds the earlier one's version of the path). A path claimed by two Changes is a conflict. **Ordering is not resolution.** A valid declared edge between the two Changes (`readSuccession`, directly from one to the other) says which lands first, and the finding records it as `ordered: true`; it does not resolve the overlap. An ordered conflict stays visible as an unresolved conflict, and so does an unordered one (`ordered: false`), until `resolved` names the path for that pair, which only a stated resolution does, and only for a pair a valid edge orders. A stale or otherwise invalid edge orders nothing. FAIL when any conflict is unresolved, naming the path and both Changes and whether it is ordered. PASS when no path is claimed by two Changes, or every such path is resolved (reported as `resolved`). UNKNOWN when `pinOf`, a Change's `claimed` or record, or `resolved` cannot be read, naming it: never "no conflict".
- **F67. Detective only.** Both reveal and gate nothing: no check name, flag or ruleset is declared here. Each control record is detective, status `designed`, `has: [change/I33]`, and declares its minimum evidence in the kinds controls asks for: runs against fixtures, at least one real run, and one provoked failure.
- **F68. An entry per control.** Each control has a thin entry in `13-source/controls/` (`run-check-predecessor.mjs`, `run-identify-conflict.mjs`) that runs its judge, `lib/check-predecessor.mjs` or `lib/identify-conflict.mjs`, through the controls adapter (`runControl`, which fails closed and logs and records the run). The entry reads the facts and prints; the judge stays pure. `recognizeIntegration` recognizes each entry by its control id. This is Change's structure Case for its own entries (Release's own adapter-structure Case asserted Release paths and was dropped with release/C161). It says only how an entry is built, not that it ran.
- **N19. Data in, verdict out.** Both judges are pure over the facts they are given. They read no file, no process and no network, use no Git and import nothing of Release. Where the facts come from (the claimed paths of other open Changes, from the trees Hosting supplies, and the records) is the caller's.

Release's acceptance expectations are kept except where the CPO ruled otherwise: a carried predecessor must be delivered, not merely match its bytes, and an ordered conflict is not passed. The rest stand: a predecessor only a Change elsewhere holds fails, and an unreadable fact is UNKNOWN.

## What Release does with it

A later small Release PLAN maps collectors to Changes, calls the two judges at PROMOTION and in the Dispatcher, wraps them in the controls adapter and decides when they run. Release's workflow wiring is Release's. That PLAN waits for this Change on main.

## Questions

1. **Order with C15.** The Cases import the library files named above and `readSuccession`. The DO of C16 follows the DO of C15 in the same collector, or after its promotion.
2. **Claimed paths as data.** Release's own-path function is not imported; the caller passes its output as `claimed`. Hosting supplies the trees of the other open collectors to the caller. Until then identify Conflict is UNKNOWN where the paths are not supplied.
3. **Ordered conflicts, settled by the CPO.** A declared edge orders the Changes and does not resolve the overlap, so an ordered conflict stays an unresolved conflict (FAIL, `ordered: true`) until synchronization establishes the resolution. How Release establishes it from a collector's tree is its own PLAN; this one takes it as the fact `resolved`.
4. **Layout.** `14-operations/controls` and `13-source/controls` are target-layout folders of a package that has not migrated, as `13-source` was for change/C14. The entries import the adapter by a path inside this repository, so they are repository tooling and not part of the shipped Skill, which stays free of KAAL terms.
5. **Ids.** C16, I33, F65 to F67, N19, G19, A39, CTL1, CTL2 and T1563 to T1569 were checked against `main` and every remote branch on 26 November 2026. Release confirms hand-picked ids.
