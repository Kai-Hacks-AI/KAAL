# Draft wording for references/transition.md

## 1. New section, after "One Change or several"

## Impact and adoption

A Change can alter what other people, systems or work already in use can do or rely on. Say so before it lands. Impact is information for a decision, not a reason to refuse a Change: a stricter rule that stops what used to be allowed may be exactly the intended outcome.

Answer from the evidence you are given:

1. **What becomes different?** The behaviour that is meant to change, including anything that was permitted and will now be refused, or the reverse.
2. **Who is affected?** The existing systems, consumers and work already under way that depend on what changes, and how many. Where a shared rule becomes stricter, name the work already under way that it would now stop, and count it.
3. **What could go wrong?** Consequences nobody intended.
4. **Does the Change's own delivery pass under the rule it changes?** If it does not, say how it is delivered instead.
5. **How does the result reach those affected?** Name when it is available (it exists and can be used), when it is integrated (those affected are connected to it) and when it is active (it takes effect for them). These may coincide or be deliberately kept apart. One step is a valid answer. No sequence is prescribed.

Mark each claim as **stated** (you were told it), **observed** (you saw it in the evidence) or **inferred** (you reasoned it). An affected party you cannot find is not known, never none.

For a small Change one line is enough, for example: no one else is affected, observed in the evidence given.

## 2. Output chapter, one added sentence at its end

When others are affected, or the result needs more than one step to reach them, also give the answers to Impact and adoption.

## 3. SKILL.md, step 7, one added sentence at its end
