# Changing KAAL

Use this to give a change to KAAL its record, and to close it. What this capability means to KAAL is stated by the Nodes `Skill` and `Changing KAAL` in the installed KAAL: read them there, by following references from Core. This file only tells you how to do the work.

## The change record

A change has its own identity, which is not a PR number. Its record is a directory inside the installed KAAL, in `<kaal-dir>/changes/` (`.kaal` is only the default name of the KAAL directory):

```
<kaal-dir>/changes/<name>/YY/MM/DD/CC/
```

`<name>` is the line of work the change belongs to; for now it is `genesis`. `YY/MM/DD` is the change date. `CC` is a two-digit sequence, `01` to `99`, identifying one change within that name and date. For example `.kaal/changes/genesis/26/10/04/02/`.

## The process

A change goes through exactly this, in this order:

```
allocate → work ⇄ review (ROWING) → seal work → retro-work, retro-owner, retro-review (WORK) → seal Change (closed)
```

Work and review go round until a review round converges on the work as it then stands; only then is the work sealed. The record holds `work/`, `review/`, `retro-work.md`, `retro-owner.md` and `retro-review.md` (the current process requires these and does not say a record may hold nothing else):

```
<kaal-dir>/changes/<name>/YY/MM/DD/CC/
├── work/              Change-local material, support and evidence; open while review has findings, frozen before the retros
├── review/            one file per round, NN.md, written by the Reviewer and never edited afterwards
├── retro-work.md      written first, by the Worker, from the Work's seat
├── retro-owner.md     written second, by the Owner, after judging the sealed Work against the Intent
└── retro-review.md    written third, by the Reviewer, from the review's seat
```

`work/` is the current convention for what is frozen before reflection, a deliberately simple boundary we are learning from. It is not a KAAL artifact type and says nothing lasting about what work is. The actual implementation lives wherever it belongs (packages, engineering, and so on), not necessarily inside `work/`; keep in `work/` the intent, requirements, architecture and the support and evidence the review and the retros are to reflect on, and do not invent file names or structure for it beyond need. `RATIFICATION` and `ROWING` (Nodes of the installed KAAL) may guide how you think while working and while reviewing; they are vocabulary, not files or phases.

Where a change is in the process is never written down. Ask `node scripts/change-state.mjs <kaal-dir> changes/<name>/YY/MM/DD/CC`: it answers where the change is, whose act is next and what it is, and what is wrong, and prints the identity of `work/` as it now stands, which a round names. It reads the record and its seals and writes nothing, and it needs only the installed KAAL and the `kaal-sealing` Skill beside this one, not this repository. You do not reconstruct that yourself. In this repository `npm run state-kaal-change -- changes/<name>/YY/MM/DD/CC` gives the same answer.

Three roles take part: the Owner, the Worker and the Reviewer, two that row and one that steers. Each is a seat: process determines whose turn it is, authority assigns the seat, and provider capability only executes the work (see Handoff and continuation and `references/rowing.md`). ROWING is the discipline of the mutable inner loop up to the Work seal; **WORK** (Worker, Owner, Reviewer, Knowledge; its meaning is the sealed KAAL Definition `WORK` beside `ROWING`, which this Skill does not redefine) is the ordered retrospective discipline after it; the sequence below is how this process realizes it. They are roles of the process; the Worker and the Reviewer of one change are never one actor (see `references/rowing.md`) for what each does and may not do, for the form of a round, and for what KAAL's checks can and cannot show.

## Steps

1. **Allocate.** `node scripts/next-change.mjs <kaal-dir> <name>` takes the number after the highest existing `CC` for that name and date, never reuses a gap, refuses when `99` is exhausted, creates the directory and prints its path relative to the KAAL directory. Do not choose a number by hand. Allocation is optimistic: the next free `CC` is the next one visible in the lineage you are working from, with no reservation, lock or registry. Concurrent work may take the same number, and that race is acceptable. It is settled at admission: if another Change has taken the address by the time you refresh against the lineage, allocate again before you seal the Work and close, and the staged Change moves there as a whole (its address is not part of its identity). Staging may race; the lineage cannot.
2. **Work.** The Worker does the change, never assigning itself or a helper it starts to the Reviewer seat, and keeps its Change-local support and evidence under `work/`, including the Intent, Requirements and Architecture the review is anchored to. The Intent is the fixed target: never change it inside the change (see `references/rowing.md`). Where the Intent was established with the optional `kaal-intent` Skill (Node `Intent`), the Worker keeps it in `work/01-intent.md` byte for byte, and its identity (`node ../kaal-intent/scripts/intent.mjs identity <file>`) is the target the rounds and the Owner's judgment are measured against: a copy in `work/` that no longer has that identity has been changed, which no one inside the change may do. Describing, checking and establishing an Intent are Intent's and happen before the change; a different want is another Intent for another change, never an edit of this one. A Change does not need Intent to work, and an Intent written otherwise is the same fixed target. Pay attention to your own experience of it; your retro is written from that. `work/` may change freely while it is open. The Worker carries the Work through its progression without returning to the Owner at each stage: starting from the described Intent it was handed (a starting point, not a stage the Worker takes part in), Requirements defined, Architecture designed, code developed, done, defects detected. A finished stage is not a stop (see Handoff and continuation). The Worker continues until the process requires Review or a genuine authority boundary is met. Completing a stage is not, by itself, a handoff; where Review falls within the progression is not settled here.
3. **Review.** The Reviewer seat is occupied by an actor assigned to it under authority the Owner granted, and never by the Worker. When the Work is ready for Review and no authorized actor occupies that seat, the process stops here: the Worker says that the Reviewer seat is empty and that the Owner's authority must assign it, and waits. It does not fill the seat itself, with a helper it starts or with any actor it merely can reach. The Reviewer reviews the Work as realized by ROWING (`references/rowing.md`) and writes the next round, `review/NN.md`, naming the identity of `work/` that `change-state` prints and stating in plain words under whose authority it occupies the seat. A round says `findings` or `converged`. With findings, the Worker resolves them in `work/` and the Reviewer writes the next round; each hands the Change to the other at that point. This repeats inside the one change until a round says `converged` about the work as it now stands. A converged round hands the Change onward: sealing the Work and writing `retro-work.md` are the Worker's, and the Worker, never the same actor as the Reviewer, does them next. The Reviewer does not change `work/`. The form of a round, and the question whether review has converged on exactly this `work/`, are Review's (the optional `kaal-review` Skill, Node `Review`): where it is installed the Reviewer writes the round with `node ../kaal-review/scripts/review.mjs write review/NN.md --of Work --identity <the work: identity> --outcome findings|converged --reviewer <statement> [--findings <text>]` and may ask `review.mjs converged review <identity>`. What Changing KAAL adds is this step: who reviews, when, and what follows. A Change does not need Review to work, and a round in that form written otherwise is read the same.
4. **Seal work.** Once the review has converged, the Worker role seals `work/` without another go: `npm run seal-kaal-work -- changes/<name>/YY/MM/DD/CC`. Treat `work/` as immutable from then on. Do this before any retro begins, so that what you reflect on cannot be revised afterwards. If it refuses or `change-state` reports a problem, resolve it; do not bypass it.
5. **Retro, from the Work's seat (first).** Once the Work is sealed, the Worker writes `retro-work.md` in the change directory, as described in `references/retro.md`. Writing it does not disturb the seal on `work/`.
6. **Retro, from the Owner's seat (second).** After the Worker's, the Owner judges whether the sealed Work answers the fixed Intent, having the Work and the Worker's perspective; that judgment is a process act, not an artifact, and nothing in KAAL records an approval or verdict. This is the one stop until the Owner's own text arrives: an actor cannot know the Owner has judged except by that text being present, since the judgment is no artifact. Then the Owner writes `retro-owner.md`, as described in `references/retro.md` (the Owner's own text may be carried into the file verbatim by another actor, which is moving it, not writing it): what the Work as it stands taught the Owner about the product and the Intent. It carries no approval or verdict, may inform a future Intent, and cannot revise this change's.
7. **Retro, from the review's seat (third).** Last, the Reviewer writes `retro-review.md`, as described in `references/retro.md`, with the Worker's and the Owner's perspectives available: what it learned, liked, lacked and longed for in reviewing. It is not the review's result, and it carries no verdict. The accumulated result of the three is Knowledge. Once the Owner's own text, `retro-owner.md`, is present (the only way an actor can know the Owner has judged), nothing between here and closing is the Owner's: the actor holding the Reviewer role writes this retro and closes.
8. **Close.** `npm run close-kaal-change -- changes/<name>/YY/MM/DD/CC`. The Change's seal covers `work/`, `review/` and the three retros together; there is no separate seal for any of them. How a host then authorizes or admits the closed change is outside KAAL, which knows nothing of host accounts, approvals or checks.

## Handoff and continuation

The human and the Owner describe the Intent together. That described Intent, handed to the Worker, is the authority to work. Once handed off, the Worker does not own or revise it. The Work record represents the described Intent; no particular file or file shape is what makes the handoff authoritative, and no file says "go". From there:

- **A transition is not a permission.** When the process makes a next act legal and you hold its role, do it. The end of a stage, a converged round or a sealed Work is not a reason to ask again.
- **Real stops are few.** Review when the process requires it and no authorized actor occupies the Reviewer seat (the process stops and names the missing seat; it never substitutes another actor); a decision that is genuinely the Owner's and unresolved; host admission, which is outside KAAL. Where Review belongs among the stages is not fixed: it is learned from ordinary Changes, not encoded here.
- **The Owner's acts stay the Owner's.** The judgment of the sealed Work against the Intent, and the text of `retro-owner.md`, are never written, inferred or supplied by anyone else, and a handoff, a green check or silence is not that judgment. Describing the Intent is co-work and is not solely the Owner's act; after the handoff, what becomes of an Intent that cannot be delivered as stated is the Owner's to decide. Carrying the Owner's own text verbatim into `retro-owner.md` is not authoring it.
- **Process determines the turn. Authority assigns the seat. Provider capability executes the work.** The process says whose act is next and what it must be. The Owner, the human, assigns seats, or delegates seat assignment to an orchestrator; delegation reaches only as far as it was explicitly granted and is never inferred from what a provider, tool, session or account can do. Having a tool that starts agents, reviews or approves is not authority to assign a seat.
- **Roles are not actors, but Worker and Reviewer are never one.** Several actors may share one Change, and one actor may hold several roles, except that it does not hold the Worker and the Reviewer seats of the same change merely because it can do both jobs. An authorized orchestrator may assign distinct actors, even through one provider; orchestration does not erase the separation of judgment. Role boundaries otherwise order whose perspective an act comes from and what it may draw on; they are knowledge boundaries, not requests for permission.
- **After the Owner's act**, known only by the Owner's own text being present, the actor holding the Reviewer role may write `retro-review.md`, close the Change and put it forward for host admission without the Owner relaying anything.

## Admission

A Change joins the admitted KAAL only closed. Many steps may go into doing it, and they happen wherever work is staged; the Change is open there and its `work/` may change freely. What is proposed for admission is the finished state: one new Change, closed, with everything it changed. A partial Change is not proposed, and one admission carries exactly one new Change. `npm run check-kaal-admission -- <baseline-kaal-dir> <candidate-kaal-dir>` answers whether a candidate may be admitted (it is the repository's provisional helper, like the others; it refuses what is not closed, and does not judge whether the Change describes the rest of the candidate faithfully). Seal before you propose, not after: nothing is added to a Change once it is admitted.

## Rules

- Do not seal work before a review round has converged on it, do not write a retro before the work is sealed, and do not go back and alter sealed work to make the history cleaner. If you find something while writing a retro, it belongs in the retro.
- The Intent is the fixed target of a change: neither the Worker, the Reviewer nor the Owner inside the loop revises it. A review that finds it cannot be delivered as stated does not converge as a success; what the Owner does then is outside the change.
- Review is not execution and not ownership: the Reviewer judges the Work as realized, against the Change as intended, and does not rewrite it. A finding is work to resolve; a broader consequence is for the retro or a future change.
- A change record is immutable once closed: never edit a sealed `work/`, a round or a retro once written, or a closed change, and never reuse a change directory or renumber a closed one.
- Each retro is its writer's own. Do not write what the user, anyone else or a team thought, and do not write what you believe is wanted. A retro does not approve, and a finding does not live only in a retro. A later retro may read the earlier ones, and the order Worker, Owner, Reviewer is required. The Owner's judgment of the sealed Work against the Intent and `retro-owner.md` are two acts: the judgment is no artifact, and the retro carries no approval.
- The Worker neither assigns nor controls the Reviewer: a helper it starts on its own side (a subagent, a fresh context, a resumed session) without an assignment to the Reviewer seat is part of the Worker, and its output is Work evidence, never a round; a distinct actor the Owner's authority assigned to the Reviewer seat writes rounds under that grant, even when the Worker prompts or resumes it. KAAL's checks see artifacts and their order, never who wrote them or under whose authority; that the Reviewer was assigned under authority and judged apart from the Worker is asked of the process, shown by the Reviewer's own statement in the round, and not something the checks can establish.
- Do not add metadata that says what phase a change is in. Other meaningful artifacts may join a change record where a capability or process calls for them; they do not replace or reorder the steps above.
- Changes closed before review was a step are valid as they are: genesis `01` and `05/01` with the single historical `retro.md`, `05/02` and `06/01` with `retro-work.md` and `retro-observe.md` and no review. Do not alter them. In a new change neither `retro.md` nor `retro-observe.md` is written: `retro-observe.md` is the historical name of the outer perspective, from when it was thought of as an Observer, and it is the Owner's retrospective, `retro-owner.md`, beside `retro-work.md` and `retro-review.md`.

## Scripts and helpers

- `scripts/next-change.mjs [--date YYYY-MM-DD] <kaal-dir> <name>`: allocates and creates the next change directory under `<kaal-dir>/changes/<name>/`, dated today in UTC unless `--date` is given; exit 0, or 1 when it refuses. `<name>` is lowercase letters, digits and single hyphens.

- `scripts/change-state.mjs <kaal-dir> <change>`: prints the stage, `next:` (the act and whose it is), `work:` (the identity of `work/`, while the change is not closed) and a `problem:` line for each problem; exit 0, or 1 when there is a problem or the change is unknown, 2 on usage. It writes nothing. It is the one definition of the order, and it asks the `kaal-sealing` Skill beside this one for identities; without it, it refuses and says so.

The sealing and closing commands are the repository's provisional helpers (`engineering/change-seal`), run from the repository root; this skill ships no script for them. `npm run seal-kaal-change` is the bare Change seal beneath `close-kaal-change` and does not know the process, so do not use it to close a change.
