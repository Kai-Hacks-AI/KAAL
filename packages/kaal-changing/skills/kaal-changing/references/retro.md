# Reference: retro-work.md, retro-owner.md and retro-review.md

A change has three retrospectives, from three perspectives. All three are written only after the change's `work/` is sealed, which is after review has converged, in a fixed order, because knowledge accumulates: the Worker's, then the Owner's, then the Reviewer's. The Change is sealed after all three. That ordered discipline is **WORK**: Worker, Owner, Reviewer, Knowledge.

1. `retro-work.md`, first, is written from the Work's seat, by the Worker that performed the now-sealed Work: the inside experience.
2. The Owner then judges, against the fixed Intent, whether the sealed Work answers it, having the Work and the Worker's perspective. That judgment is a process act and is no artifact: it is not recorded in KAAL, and no approval or verdict is encoded.
3. `retro-owner.md`, second, is written from the Owner's seat, by the Owner, who holds the Intent and stayed outside the work and review that went round inside it, after that judgment.
4. `retro-review.md`, third, is written from the review's seat, by the Reviewer that reviewed the Work (`rowing.md`), once the Worker's and the Owner's perspectives are available.
5. The accumulated result is Knowledge. Then the Change can be sealed.

The Worker and the Reviewer are the two that row, each from its own oar; the Owner steers, and observes from the helm.

A later retro may, and is expected to, read the earlier ones: accumulation is the point, and avoiding that reading is not the goal. Each is still the writer's own: they are different seats, not a draft and a correction, and each reports its own seat's experience. The order is required, and KAAL's evaluator requires it by the artifacts present; who wrote a file is not something it can show.

A retrospective is not the review, and the Owner's retrospective is not the Owner's judgment: `retro-owner.md` is written after that judgment and carries no approval or verdict. What a host does to authorize or admit the closed Change is outside KAAL and outside this record, and KAAL knows nothing of its accounts, approvals or checks. The review's result is in the rounds in `review/`: findings to resolve, or convergence. A retro carries what its writer learned, liked, lacked and longed for, and never a verdict, an approval or a finding that exists nowhere else.

## Form

Each file has exactly this form, and nothing else:

```
# Retro

## Learned

...

## Liked

...

## Lacked

...

## Longed

...
```

No metadata, scores, action items, owners, identity of the writer, role labels or summary.

## Whose retro it is

`retro-work.md` is written from the worker's own seat and reports the worker's own experience of performing the change.

`retro-review.md` is written from the Reviewer's own seat and reports the Reviewer's own experience of reviewing the Work: what it learned, liked, lacked and longed for in doing so. Written last, it may draw on the Worker's and the Owner's retros, and it does not speak for either. The Reviewer may have approved the Work and still have substantial criticism here; what it saw beyond this change (broader consequences, future work) belongs here, not in a finding.

`retro-owner.md` is written from the Owner's own seat (its text is the Owner's: another actor may carry that text into the file verbatim, which is moving it, not writing it), after the Owner's judgment of the sealed Work against the Intent, and so carries no approval or verdict. It is deliberately shaped by the Owner's position: what the Work, as it stands, taught the Owner about the product and the Intent, what it learned, liked, lacked and longed for as the one whose Intent was realized. That learning may inform a future Intent; it cannot revise this Change's fixed Intent. It does not restate the review's result.

Neither infers what the user thought, speaks for anyone else, invents anyone's sentiment, or says what is expected to be welcome. Where the writer has nothing honest to say for a dimension, it says so plainly rather than filling the space.

## The four dimensions

They are distinct in meaning, in all three retros.

- **Learned**: knowledge or understanding the writer gained that was not known or clear at the beginning.
- **Liked**: something that worked well, from the writer's perspective, and is worth preserving.
- **Lacked**: something absent whose absence made the writer's experience harder, weaker, slower or less certain.
- **Longed**: a desired future possibility or improvement, from the writer's perspective. It is not a restatement of something Lacked.

Do not take one observation and write it four ways. If an observation could fit several dimensions, put it in the one that is its primary meaning and find different observations for the others. Each dimension holds observations the others do not.

## The historical forms

Changes closed before the perspectives hold a single `retro.md` of the same form. Changes closed before review was a step hold `retro-work.md` and `retro-observe.md`, the second written from outside the Work, of the same form; `retro-observe.md` was the name of the outer perspective then, before it was understood to be the Owner's, and no Observer role exists. They stay valid as sealed and are never rewritten. A new change writes neither `retro.md` nor `retro-observe.md`: its retrospectives are `retro-work.md`, `retro-owner.md` and `retro-review.md`, in that order.
