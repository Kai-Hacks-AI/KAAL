# research/F4 exposure and claims

Each run says whether the treatment reached the agent: `opened` (an excerpt in the evidence shows the agent opening it), `delivered`, `unchecked`, or `none`. Each claim has an `id`, a `scope`, the runs it `rests-on`, whether it is `demonstrated` as `yes`, `partly` or `no`, and, unless `yes`, what is `not-shown`. The scope says how much is claimed: `description` (what these runs did), `comparison` (the arms differed in these runs) or `effect` (the treatment caused it). Stronger conditions belong to stronger scopes, and only to a claim marked `yes`:

- `comparison` rests on runs from at least two arms;
- `effect` rests on untreated and treated runs, with blind scoring, a key frozen before the treatment and named as evidence, and every treated run it rests on `opened`.

The verdict says what it is about (`verdict-about`, a scope) and which claims it rests on (`verdict-rests-on`). Its subject is the `question`; its scope is the strongest scope among those claims; and `favourable` needs every claim it rests on to be `yes`. Other claims may stay unresolved, each with its own not-shown. `unfavourable` and `inconclusive` carry no such bar. `Limits` states what the run count can and cannot show, with no threshold. The result of the judgement says it is about form only.

**Done when** a record meeting this is `well-formed`, and each of these is `malformed`: an effect claim marked `yes` without an untreated arm, without blind scoring, without the key, or on a treated run not `opened`; a comparison claim marked `yes` on one arm; a `partly` or `no` claim with nothing not shown; a claim with no id, an unknown scope or an unknown run; no claim at all; no statement of what the count shows; an excerpt not in the evidence; a verdict with no scope or resting on no or an unknown claim, with a scope that is not the strongest of its claims, or `favourable` on a claim not shown. A narrow `favourable` verdict beside an unresolved claim, an honest `partly` effect claim on an unchecked run, and an `unfavourable` verdict on an undemonstrated claim are `well-formed`. The result carries a statement that it is form only, and says `unjudged` for a file that is no rehearsal.
