# controls/C23 a ratchet is a strategy of a preventive control

C21 registers a ratchet as a third station mode (`Integrating:ratchet`). The CPO's direction and the handover of 23 November say otherwise: control types stay directive, detective, preventive and corrective; a ratchet is a temporary strategy of a preventive control, not a type and not a peer registry mode.

1. **Stations take two modes again.** `stations: [<Station>:<mode>]` accepts `detective` and `preventive`. `X:ratchet` is refused with a recovery that points at the strategy.
2. **A ratcheted control declares its strategy on the control.** `strategy: ratchet` (the only value), and with it `baseline-source` (where the accepted baseline is read from: accepted state, never the change under review), `owner` and `exit` (what it becomes when the baseline is empty). It must be a preventive control. Any of the three missing, or `strategy` on another type, is a listed problem with a recovery. `exit`, `baseline-source` or `owner` without the strategy is also listed.
3. **The script keeps its `ratchet` selector.** `judge-mode.mjs --mode ratchet` stays the way an adopter applies the strategy; it is the internal selector of the shared mechanics, not a registration mode. The Skill says so in the existing words of `modes.md` and `enforcing-controls.md`, with no new Skill length.
4. **Out of the generic contract:** deadlines and scheduling (planning's), and registration and enforcement of a real control such as release/CTL19 (release's own Change, C130). The generic mechanics, the accepted baseline and no-regression behaviour stay controls'.

Target documents F34 item 1 and Case T1110 change on purpose; `change.md` records it.

## The distinction this Change preserves

Three distinct pieces make a ratchet work, and C23 declares only the second.

1. A **detective control** identifies the current violations. It is the evidence of the state: the ratchet judges the findings it supplies, and a ratchet without that evidence cannot judge (the script answers UNKNOWN, C22).
2. A **preventive control** uses the ratchet to prevent regression against the accepted baseline. This is the control that declares `strategy: ratchet` (C23).
3. **Correction** needs at least one properly registered Change, perhaps several, aimed at eliminating the identified violations. Completing a Change is not evidence that a violation has gone: the detective control establishes the resulting state, and the baseline shrinks only when its findings do.

How the ratchet's findings link to those corrective Changes is not defined here. C23 adds no field, check or Requirement for it; that is a separate follow-up PLAN (named below), and the CPO's request is that it not be absorbed into this one.
