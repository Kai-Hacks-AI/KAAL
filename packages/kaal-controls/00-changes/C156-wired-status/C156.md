# controls/C156 a pure judge tells which controls are wired, code-only or wired but unproven

Kai asked whether controls has machinery that knows what is wired. Controls has judges that compare claims with facts (verify Record, demand Evidence, verify Adapter), but nothing that says, for the controls an adopter has declared, which are installed in its delivery, which exist only as code, and which are installed without proof that they run. The answer is one pure judgement over three inputs the adopter supplies; it reads no workflow itself.

**The function.** `judgeWired({declared, installed, running, revision, scope})` in `scripts/judge-wired.mjs`, pure. `declared` and `installed` are two inventories `{revision, scope, complete, entries}` as in controls/F207: the controls declared (`{id, path?}`) and the entries the adopter observed installed in the delivery. `running` is the list of control ids whose running is proven for this revision (the adopter's own proof, such as controls/F203's `executed`).

**What it says.** For every declared control, one status:
1. `code-only`: declared and not observed installed. It waits for a WIRE request, and it is not a nonconformance.
2. `wired-unproven`: observed installed and not proven running.
3. `wired`: observed installed and proven running.

The answer is a list `{control, status}` sorted by control. It is a report, never a finding: nothing here is a nonconformance, nothing is baselined, and nothing blocks.

**UNKNOWN.** An inventory that is missing, malformed, not complete, or of another revision or scope, a declaration without an id or declared twice, an installed or running entry that is not text, a running id that nobody declared, and a running id that is not observed installed make the judge throw, so the adapter answers UNKNOWN for the whole report. A control is never given a status from evidence that cannot be trusted.

## Not here

How the delivery is discovered (the workflows on main, the delivery map, a byte-equal WIRE check), how running is proven and where the report appears are the adopter's: release's runner and report, a separate PLAN. An installed entry that no declaration matches is verify Delivery's finding, and a status that lags or overclaims a declared claim is verify Record's. Nothing is implemented before the go on this PLAN, and nothing is wired. See `change.md`.
