# controls/C144 verify Record finds that the record of a control agrees with its evidence

One small control with one property, built on the library of controls/C141 (the adapter and `deriveStatus`). Asked for by Kai: small, meaningful controls, each following the change protocol until it blocks.

**The control.** `controls/CTL3`, `verify Record`, type detective, status designed. For every control in `controls` (`{id, status, evidence}`), `deriveStatus` of the library (C141) gives a finding; it is listed as `record-lags:<id>` when the evidence shows more than the claim and `overclaims:<id>` when the claim shows more than the evidence. A claim of `implemented` is never listed. It is the one check of the answer "does the record agree with the evidence", flagging both directions, and it is the backstop on a promotion into main.

**The check.** `scripts/verify-record.mjs` exports `findDisagreeing({controls, records})`, pure. It returns a sorted list of unique names, empty when nothing is found, and throws on an input that is not a list, so the adapter fails closed with `UNKNOWN`. It reads no file and knows no repository.

**The rollout, by the change protocol.**
1. **Detective.** The check reports through the adapter and blocks nothing.
2. **Advisory.** The adopter runs it as an advisory check behind its own flag, and evidence is collected: Cases on fixtures, real runs on pull requests, and a provoked failure.
3. **Preventive.** Only on the Owner's explicit go, and only with the provoked-fail evidence the protocol asks for. Since controls that exist today would fail, it runs as a ratchet (C24, C25): their names form the baseline, shrink with a history as release migrates them, and the ratchet ends at zero.

## Not here

Wiring the control as a station or a required check, the flag, the ruleset, and migrating the controls it finds are release's. Nothing is implemented before the Owner's go on this PLAN.

## Revised after the CPO clarification

The check compares each declared claim with its own fact, one by one, instead of one derived status. This follows the four independent facts of controls/C141 (defined, implemented, running, tested): each has its own proof for the specific control and revision, none implies another, and missing evidence is UNKNOWN (the check throws, so the adapter fails closed), never a clean verdict and never proof of absence.
