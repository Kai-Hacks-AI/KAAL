# controls/I34 the package practises what it ships

Controls ships one way of doing controls: a control is declared, its four facts are recorded, and a record that lags or overclaims its proof is found by name. The package has five controls of its own (CTL1 to CTL5), and none of them is judged by that mechanism: each is recorded as `designed` although its check exists and is tested, and nothing in the package says so. A product that asks adopters to keep their records true while its own records are not is not yet a reusable product, and its first adopter, itself, would show the gaps before anyone else does.

The intent is that the package applies its own check of records (controls/CTL3) to its own controls, from its own files and with nothing outside the package, and that its own records agree with their proof. What the proof of a fact is stays what the controls already say; this intent adds no new rule for adopters. It does not wire, enforce or install anything: running the checks at a station is the Owner's decision and stays with the change protocol.
