# controls/F10 four-types

A control is directive, preventive, detective or corrective, and the type says what it is for.

- **Directive** states the required behavior. It may or may not be followed.
- **Preventive** blocks a prohibited action, for a named actor or interface, within a named enforcement boundary.
- **Detective** reveals a deviation. It blocks nothing.
- **Corrective** restores the desired state after a deviation.

The type is told by what the control does when the rule is broken, not by its name, its mechanism or how firmly it is worded. Type is a matter of design: it does not change with what has been observed.
