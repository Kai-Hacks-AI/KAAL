# controls/F209 demand-record

1. `scripts/demand-record.mjs` exports `findRecordDue({discrepancies, observed, revision})`, pure. It owns **when** a correction is due. It makes no status comparison of its own: `discrepancies` is the answer of `findDisagreeing` (C144), the names `<fact>:lags:<id>` and `<fact>:overclaims:<id>`.
2. Four independent facts are distinguished by when their proof exists. **Before a WIRE**: defined, implemented, and the testing applicable before installation. **Only after a WIRE**: actual installation, running, and operational testing. The precise evidence each needs is the rollout protocol's, not restated here.
3. `observed` is the list of observations `{control, revision, installed}` of the actual delivery, `true` or `false`, independently of the declaration, and counting only for its control and the applicable `revision`.
4. A discrepancy on a fact that exists before a WIRE is never made due here: it is C144's finding on the owner's pull request. A discrepancy on `running` or `tested` of a control observed installed at the revision is `record-due:<fact>:<id>`: the documentation is due on the owning capability's next applicable pull request into its collector, because installing is a WIRE, which cannot write package files. A discrepancy on those facts of a control observed not installed is not yet due, and is neither a finding nor a false claim.
5. A control named by a discrepancy with no observation for the revision, malformed input, or a discrepancy name that is not `<fact>:<lags|overclaims>:<id>` makes the check throw, so that the adapter fails closed with `UNKNOWN` and no clean result is given from missing evidence.
6. The answer is a sorted list of unique names. It reads no file and writes nothing, and knows no repository, path, flag or service.
