# review/C6 inspector-app

Review owns the KAAL Inspector, the GitHub App that signs one check per registered control, and, in a later PLAN, the KAAL Approver. This Change takes the Inspector over from release/C102, whose PLAN (#2121) was open since 20 November and never reviewed by the CPO, and re-homes it under Review. It is the first bounded increment: what the Inspector declares it needs, the rule that no App decides on what it raised, the judgement of its workflow, and who owns what. The Approver gets its own PLAN.

## Baseline

`main` holds the concept of the Inspector in controls (`packages/kaal-controls/00-changes/C14-control-enforcement-unit/design.md`: a judge step with no secret, a sign step with the only secret, one check per registered control, a ruleset that pins the check to the App). No manifest, permission list or workflow of the App exists, and no required check is pinned to an App. Release's PLAN #2121 drafted, under `engineering/release-management`, an Intent (I55), a Requirement (F79), an Architecture (A70), a manifest `app.json`, a workflow `inspector.yml` and five Cases (T1250 to T1254), all RED.

The Owner ruled on 26 November that Review owns the Inspector and the Approver, that Release keeps only the Dispatcher (which may later dispatch more pull request types than PROMOTION), that Hosting owns the App adapter for all three (manifests, tokens, API calls, logos), and that the Owner creates and installs each App, stores its key and draws its logo by hand.

### The contracts

- **Authority (F32).** One declaration per App, `13-source/github-apps/<app>.json`, read as data: `app`, `name`, `owner`, `purpose`, `abilities` and optionally `logo`. For the Inspector the abilities are exactly `post-checks`, `read-contents` and `read-pull-requests`. An ability is a neutral word from Hosting's closed list (`read-contents`, `read-pull-requests`, `raise-pull-requests`, `approve-pull-requests`, `read-checks`, `post-checks`); Hosting maps each to what GitHub grants, renders the manifest and mints the token, and refuses a call whose token lacks the ability.
- **No App decides on what it raised (F33).** Across declarations, `approve-pull-requests` is held by the Approver only, and no App holds both `raise-pull-requests` and `approve-pull-requests`. Hosting refuses a declaration that holds both; the rule that only the Approver may approve is Review's. GitHub cannot tell the two apart, which is why they are separate abilities.
- **Separation (F34).** The workflow is judged as text. The judge job holds only `contents: read`, no secret, no kept credentials, and checks out the base; the sign job needs the judge, checks out only the base, takes the verdicts as data and alone names the key. Nothing starts from write permissions or uses `pull_request_target`.
- **UNKNOWN** never signs as a pass; this is Controls' sign step, and the workflow only carries it.

### Ownership

A3 is the table. In short: Review owns the Inspector's and the Approver's declared authority and the judgement of it; Release owns the Dispatcher; Hosting owns the adapter and the ability list; Controls owns the judge, the sign step and the checks; the Owner creates, installs, stores keys and draws logos by hand. No WIRE and no `.github` file is made here, and a WIRE needs the Owner's go.

## Boundaries

- **Hosting** (hosting/C114) owns the closed ability list, the mapping to GitHub permissions, the manifests, the tokens, the calls and the logo slots. Hosting's calls for posting checks and approving are not planned yet; this Change asks for them when its DO needs them.
- **Controls** owns the judge and the sign step; `judgeWorkflow` only checks that the workflow keeps them apart. C22 and the registered controls are not touched.
- **Release** owns the Dispatcher and the PR conventions it reads. Its declaration is its own. Release's C153 (#2601) depends on the Approver for approve Sync, review Sync and the Promotion Source check; the Approver is not in this increment.
- **Engineering** owns the layout. `13-source` does not exist in this package yet; adding these files is the first step of a move to the layout that stays a separate Change.
