# C1 act

A 4L retrospective of the Change, from the viewpoint of the agent that carried it.

## Liked

The reviews of the PLAN did more than the PLAN itself. Each time the CPO or Kai moved the line, from a Skill-and-trial review to a review by focus, and then to judgement against proof, the PLAN got smaller and more general, and the second and third rewrites cost little because the requirements were few. Writing the Cases first, as failing, made each rewrite checkable: after every change I ran all nine and watched them fail for the right reason. Breaking a copy of a record on purpose showed that T24 and T28 catch what they claim to catch.

## Learned

I first tied Review to its first subject. A requirement that names a subject reads as design and then has to be unpicked, so subject-specific facts belong in the data of one application and nowhere else. I also first gave the reviewer a trial of its own, which was proof dressed as review. The test of a finding is whether an oracle exists: if one does, a test proves it and the review points at it, and if none does, the review judges and says why. A Case that checks a length rewards length, so a Case should check the structure that makes a comparison a comparison. The first Branching run on a new PR fails before auto-merge registers, and the next run passes.

## Lacked

A second pair of eyes on the records. The reviewer is the Agent that wrote the PLAN, and the Cases check shape and cited evidence, not whether a judgement is sound. I also lacked a way to see the results of the Architecture Cases, which sit outside CI, so those findings rest on the author's record.

## Longed For

A review of a review by another Agent, with no sight of mine first, so that disagreement is visible before the Owner reads either. A Case that a promotion's changed paths fall under the declared Changes, which the promotion review marked testable and not tested. A calibration of judged findings against a human, which neither this Change nor its neighbour has.
