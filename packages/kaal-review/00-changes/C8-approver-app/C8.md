# review/C8 approver-app

Review owns the KAAL Approver, the GitHub App that approves a clean SYNC. The Owner ruled that approval is given by an App, not a person or a bot account, and that the Approver is owned by Review and built by Hosting's App adapter. review/C6 took over the Inspector; this Change is the Approver's own PLAN, the first bounded increment: what the Approver declares it needs, and the pure decision to approve or abstain. Release's C153 (`approve Sync`, `review Sync`) and the approving rule of release/C66 wait on it. A PLAN: no DO is authorized, and no App, key, workflow, `.github` file, WIRE or ruleset is made.

## Baseline

`kaal/review/inspector-app` holds the C6 PLAN: the Inspector's declaration contract (F32), the rule that no App decides on what it raised (F33, which already names the Approver as the one App that may hold `approve-pull-requests`), the workflow separation (F34) and provenance (F35). Nothing yet says what the Approver itself needs or when it may approve. Today approvals of a SYNC are a person's or the sync bot's (release/CTL8), and release/C153 says `approve Sync` and `review Sync` need an App that does not exist.

## PLAN

| Requirement | Intent | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| review/F39 | review/I11 | the declaration `approver.json` with exactly four abilities, read like the Inspector's, and the two together under F33 |

Architecture is review/A5. The Cases are review/T1587, review/T1588 and review/T1589 under `test/plan/approver-app/`, plain `.test.mjs`.

### Decided by the CTO

1. **The pure-merge fact** is supplied by Hosting through the C114 App adapter, in line with the ownership cut in which Hosting supplies facts and consumers judge them. `read-contents` stays in the Approver's declaration only if the adapter needs it under the Approver's identity. This PLAN pins the declaration with `read-contents` for now; the DO drops the ability, and T1587 follows, if Hosting's adapter does not need it under the Approver's identity.
2. **The trust boundary.** The Approver's workflow executes only code from main, never candidate code. It runs on a trusted trigger, judges from facts and never checks out the head. A missing proof of trusted execution is an abstention. F41 and T1589 carry it as evidence of execution; the workflow and its proof at WIRE are later.

### Contracts and design choices

- **Two answers only.** `approve` or `abstain`. The Approver never declines, merges, closes, comments or dismisses, because each of those is authority it does not need and a request for changes is a person's judgement. Abstaining leaves the pull request for a person.
- **Kind from content.** A SYNC is a pure merge of the base, a fact Hosting supplies from the bytes. Titles, bodies and branch names are never read, as Kai ruled for the Dispatcher.
- **One revision.** The evidence, the check and the decision all name the same head. A later push moves the head and the approval is asked for again; the ruleset already dismisses a stale approval.
- **Never what it raised.** F33 stops one App holding both abilities; F40 adds that an approval of a pull request whose author is the Approver abstains.
- **Declared authority.** `approve-pull-requests`, `read-checks`, `read-contents`, `read-pull-requests`. `read-contents` is present only if Hosting's evidence for the pure-merge fact needs the App's own token; this is open below.

### Expectations for the DO

Testing expectations (defined before the implementation): review/T1587 to review/T1589, which the DO turns green by adding `13-source/github-apps/approver.json`, `13-source/scripts/judge-approval.mjs` and the second argument of `readDeclaration`, and by running the Cases from `scripts.test` in place of the temporary `run-red.mjs`. The DO needs the DO of review/C6 (`judge-app.mjs` and the Inspector's declaration) on the collector first. Review expectations, as judgements and not Cases: that the four abilities are the least the approval needs, that nothing in the Skill names a KAAL App, and that no authority beyond approval is smuggled in.

### What stays manual, and what is scripted

| Manual (an Agent or a person judges) | Scripted (it must repeat exactly) |
| --- | --- |
| deciding what the Approver's authority is | reading its declaration |
| deciding what makes a SYNC clean, and whether a SYNC is wanted | deciding approve or abstain from the evidence |
| creating and installing the App, storing its key, drawing its logo (the Owner) | the set of declarations under F33 |

## Boundaries

- **Hosting** owns the closed ability list, the manifest, the token, the evidence of the pure merge, and the call that approves. Its calls for approving are not planned yet; this Change asks for them when the DO needs them.
- **Release** owns what a clean SYNC is, `verify Sync`, and the wiring of `approve Sync` and `review Sync`. It asks, Review delivers.
- **The CTO** owns the trust boundary as decided above: the Approver's workflow, that nothing the decided change contains can alter what runs it or reach its key, and the proof at WIRE. Review's F34 and F35 give the judgement of the text and of provenance; the proof is not in this Change.
- **Controls** owns the check the Approver reads and the Inspector signs it.
