# review/C5 review-items

Review owns the reusable capability for defining, performing and recording assessments, as the counterpart of what Testing owns for verification. Testing makes verification obligations and observations traceable. This Change makes validation obligations and judgements equally traceable, while keeping apart an observed result, an assessed conclusion and an authorized decision. It is the first bounded increment: the contracts, the navigation from a subject to its assessments, and two worked examples from controls/C21. A PLAN: no Skill text, no script and no change to any other package is delivered here; no DO is authorized by this PLAN.

The relationship is:

```text
Test Case ── verifies ──→ subject ←── validates ── Review item
```

The two are symmetrical in structure and not in scope. Anything can be validated, and not everything can be verified, so `validates` points at more than `tests` can: an Intent, a Requirement, an Architecture element, a Defect or code, and also a Skill, an instruction file, a document, another Review item or a result. Review keeps no list of kinds.

## Baseline

`main` holds `kaal-review` 0.0.1: the focused review of review/C1 (a free-form record with a focus, a reference and an actual) and the standalone proof of review/C2. A review today is performed and recorded in one step, in a record under `00-changes/.../applications/`; nothing is defined before it is performed, nothing points from a subject to the reviews of it, and a judgement made at one revision has no place to say whether it still holds at the next. Controls/C21 kept five judgments (J1 to J5) in a Change-local table, and their appraisals live in PR reviews. That shows the concept and leaves definitions, relationships and results less navigable than their testing counterparts. The layout of a package already reserves `02-learnings/reviews`, ids `RV<n>`, for reviews worth keeping, and says that kaal-review decides what is worthy and what verifies one.

### Testing's structure and its Review counterpart

The full table is in A2. In short: a Case `T<n>` with `tests` becomes an item `V<n>` with `validates`; `collectCases` becomes `navigate`, with the same contract (read as text, a malformed file is a finding and is never dropped); a run report becomes an execution, but kept, because an appraisal cannot be run again on demand; GREEN, RED and UNKNOWN become `satisfied`, `not-satisfied` and `unresolved`, with a run that could not finish becoming `could-not-perform` apart; an expected-revision mismatch makes everything UNKNOWN in Testing, and here makes a result `stale` unless a later record carries it forward with its reason. The differences that matter are the wider targets, the kept executions and the explicit carry-forward.

### The contracts

- **Review item (F27).** `V<n>.md`: `id` (`<namespace>/V<n>`), `validates` (one or more subjects, each an id as its owner states it or `path:<repository path>`), and the chapters Question, Criteria and Expected evidence. It exists before any assessment and names no result.
- **Result (F29).** `RV<n>.md`: front matter `id`, `appraises`, `revision`, `appraiser`, `relation` (`author`, `not-author` or `unknown`), `execution` (`performed` or `could-not-perform`), and for a performed one `conclusion` and an optional list of `parts`; chapters Execution (evidence seen, what was not done) and Result (reasoning, limitations, a disposition addressed to the owner). Definition, execution and result are three things.
- **Conclusions and applicability (F30).** `satisfied`, `not-satisfied`, `unresolved`; kept apart from a missing record (never ran), `could-not-perform` (tried, with a reason) and missing evidence (`unresolved`, naming it). A result applies to its revision and is `carried` to another only by a record with `carries` and a stated reason; otherwise `stale`. A conclusion other than `unresolved` is refused while a part is unresolved or not satisfied, so one unassessed responsibility stays visible.
- **No authority and independence (F31).** A result informs and decides nothing; a record that states an approval or a merge is a finding. The relation of appraiser to author is recorded on every performed result and Review does not say when independence is required.
- **Consumer independence (N5).** Nothing in the core names a station, a collector, a pull request host or a layout; the navigation reads a bare directory through Hosting alone. An adapter supplies those.

The terms are the CTO's choice and are proposals for the CPO's review: the letters `V` and `RV`, the hyphenated conclusion tokens, and `could-not-perform`.

### Navigation (F28)

`navigate(root, { revision })` answers from the tree alone: which items validate a subject; which items have no result that applies to the revision; which results apply, and which are carried or stale. It answers with facts and no coverage verdict, because a pointer is not coverage and which assessments are required is the subject owner's policy. Over the worked examples it shows, at the revision of C21's approval, that the appraisal of J1 is carried with its reason and that of J3 is the one made after the trial, and at the earlier revision that J3 was unresolved.

### Worked examples

`examples/` holds J1 and J3 of controls/C21 as items V1 and V2 and their four appraisals as results RV1 to RV4, with the PR review each came from. They are history and not new executions: the words are the appraiser's and the only addition is the mapping of the stated disposition into the three conclusions, which each result says. Nothing in controls changes. Two things they show: RV3 keeps visible what the original said in one clause, "satisfied on the documents; not evidenced by the trial", as a satisfied part and an unresolved part, so the whole is `unresolved` until the trial; and RV2 carries J1 over a later commit that does not touch it, saying why. The CPO's approval of the DO is a separate decision that cites these results.

### Placement

An item sits beside the subject it points at, in a folder `review-items` next to `test-cases`; results sit in `02-learnings/reviews`, which the layout already reserves. The reader takes a root and assumes neither. Adding `review-items` to the layout of a package belongs to Engineering and is asked for, not made here. Where a capability has instances (the C21 judgments in controls), they stay with their domain; Review owns the mechanism and not the placement of every domain's items.

### What stays manual, and what is scripted

| Manual (an Agent or a person judges) | Scripted (it must repeat exactly) |
| --- | --- |
| writing an item: the question, the criteria, the expected evidence | reading the tree and listing the items and results |
| performing an appraisal and choosing the conclusion | the reverse pointer, who validates a subject |
| deciding that a carry-forward is justified | classifying a result as applies, carried or stale for a revision |
| judging whether the pointers are enough | reporting a malformed file, an incoherent record or an authority claim |
| deciding whether an independent appraisal is required | none: the relation is recorded and read by whoever owns the policy |

Only the navigation warrants a script, `scripts/navigate.mjs` in the Skill folder, with a Skill reference for the conventions. The Agent level (`AGENTS.md` of the package, then the shipped one) is left to Engineering and Agents; this increment adds none.

## Boundaries

- **Testing** owns Cases, the catalog and the run report. This Change reads none of them, imports nothing from it and asks Testing for nothing; the Judgement loop of its Skill already points a claim that only judgement can settle to a recorded judgement from another seat, which is where Review's results begin. If Testing later offers a general reader of such trees, Review may use it; today it mirrors the contract.
- **Controls** owns controls and where they are required. C22 is not expanded; C21's judgments are used as history. A control's wish for `required-at` is an adapter's field beside the item.
- **Research** (C1, #2324) owns the method of trial evidence. An item may name a trial as expected evidence and no more.
- **Engineering** owns the layout (`review-items`), and the Agents capability the Agent level.
- **The earlier reviews** (applications of review/C1, C3 and C4) stay as they are: a free-form review without a prior item is still a review; this adds the predefined kind beside it.
