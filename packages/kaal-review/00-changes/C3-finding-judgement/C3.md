# review/C3 finding-judgement

A finding raised against a piece of work, by a human or by an automated reviewer such as `@codex review`, is judged before anything acts on it. A valid finding is not automatically owned by the work it was found in. Review's part is the judgement and the stop: it challenges the finding, says whether it is valid, whether it can be proven by a test or can only be judged, and whose Change the failing element belongs to, and then it hands over. It fixes nothing, runs no test, records no defect and decides nothing. A first slice: one new focus for the existing review record; no change to the Skill, to the Nodes or to any other package; no DO is authorized by this PLAN.

## Baseline

`main` holds `kaal-review` 0.0.1 with the focused review (review/C1), the standalone proof (review/C2) and the rule that a finding is marked Proven, Judged or Testable, not tested. Any focus not defined by Review carries its own reference and actual. Review relies on no Skill of another capability and names no subject.

### Review's part, as SIPOC

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Supplier | whoever raised a finding, human or Agent |
| Input | one finding, the work it concerns and the declared scope of that work's Change |
| Process | read where the finding was found, which the finding itself gives; challenge the finding against the work, not for its own sake; decide valid, invalid or unknown; mark it Proven (a Case is cited), Judged, or Testable, not tested; judge separately where it belongs to be fixed, inside the Change's declared scope or outside it |
| Output | a judged finding: a Verdict with its challenge and evidence and the mark, and two distinct facts, where it was found and where it belongs to be fixed |
| Customer | whoever owns the work, and whoever must act on what the Verdict says |

Review stops at the judged finding. What follows is handed over in three abstract forms, with no named destination:
- **to fix in this Change**: the failing element is inside the declared scope, so the Change's author fixes it before the Change is accepted;
- **to prove**: the finding is valid and deterministic, so a claim is handed over for someone who owns proof to make RED;
- **to record**: the finding is valid and its failing element lies outside the declared scope, so a description of the defect, with where it was found and what shows it, is handed over for someone who owns the record of defects.

A valid deterministic finding can need all of "to prove" and "to record". The record states which handovers apply and why. It does not say who takes them.

### Two facts, not one

Where a finding was found and where it belongs to be fixed are different facts and the record keeps them apart. The first is free: a reviewer such as `@codex review` places its finding on a file and line, and the record copies that placement as given. The second is a judgement, made by Review: the failing element may be in the file where the finding sits, or somewhere that the work only touches. A finding found inside a Change is not therefore to be fixed by it; the record of the defect and its Case belong with the finding either way, and the fix belongs with whoever owns the failing element.

## Flow ownership (a proposal, not a decision)

Review owns the judgement and nothing after it. The end-to-end flow, from a finding being raised to a defect being recorded, proven and routed to the owner of the failing element, crosses at least four concerns that each stop at their own boundary: the judgement here, the proof of a claim, the record of defects, and the Change that is being accepted. Nothing owns the sequence between them. I propose that the flow step that watches pull requests on their way in (the integrating flow) owns that sequence, since it is where findings arrive and where the Change is held until they are dealt with, and that each capability keeps only its own step. That is for the Owner to decide; this Change assumes no answer.
