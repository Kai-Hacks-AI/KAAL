# review/C3 finding-judgement

A finding raised against a piece of work, by a human or by an automated reviewer such as `@codex review`, is judged before anything acts on it. Review has the finding: it challenges it, says whether it is valid, whether a test can prove it or it can only be judged, and where it was found, and then it hands over. It does not decide where a valid finding is to be fixed, which is a matter of functional placement and not Review's, and it fixes nothing, runs no test and records no defect. A first slice: one new focus for the existing review record; no change to the Skill, to the Nodes or to any other package; no DO is authorized by this PLAN.

## Baseline

`main` holds `kaal-review` 0.0.1 with the focused review (review/C1), the standalone proof (review/C2) and the rule that a finding is marked Proven, Judged or Testable, not tested. Any focus not defined by Review carries its own reference and actual. Review relies on no Skill of another capability and names no subject.

### Review's part, as SIPOC

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Supplier | whoever raised a finding, human or Agent |
| Input | one finding, with the place it was raised on, and the work it concerns |
| Process | read where the finding was found, which the finding itself gives; challenge the finding against the work, not for its own sake; decide valid, invalid or unknown; mark it Proven (a Case is cited), Judged, or Testable, not tested |
| Output | a judged finding: a Verdict with its challenge, evidence and mark, and where it was found |
| Customer | whoever owns the work, and whoever must act on what the Verdict says |

Review stops at the judged finding. What follows is handed over in three abstract forms, with no named destination:
- **to prove**: the finding is valid and deterministic, so a claim is handed over for someone who owns proof to make RED;
- **to record**: the finding is valid, so a description of the defect, with where it was found and what shows it, is handed over for someone who owns the record of defects;
- **to place**: the finding is valid and it is not settled that the failing element is where the finding was found, so it is handed over for someone who owns functional placement to decide where the fix belongs.

A valid deterministic finding can need all three. The record states which handovers apply and why. It does not say who takes them.

### Found at is a fact; where to fix is not Review's

Where a finding was found is free: a reviewer such as `@codex review` places its finding on a file and line, and the record copies that placement as given. Where the fix belongs is a different question and Review does not answer it. Until placement says otherwise, the defect stays where it was found, and a finding found in a Change is not thereby to be fixed by it, nor is it thereby not to be.

## Flow ownership (a proposal, not a decision)

Review has the finding; the mechanic of recording defects, the mechanics of running proof and the functional placement each have an owner of their own, and each stops at its boundary. Nothing owns the sequence between them, from a finding being raised to the defect resting where it belongs. I propose that the flow step that watches pull requests on their way in (the integrating flow) owns that sequence, since it is where findings arrive and where the work is held until they are dealt with, and that each capability keeps only its own step. That is for the Owner to decide; this Change assumes no answer.
