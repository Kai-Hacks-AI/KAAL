# review/A5 approval

| Element | Owner | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the Approver's declared authority and the decision to approve | Review | `13-source/github-apps/approver.json`, `readDeclaration(text, app)` in `judge-app.mjs` (the Inspector stays the default), and the pure judgement `judgeApproval(evidence, expected)` in `13-source/scripts/judge-approval.mjs` |
| the pure-merge fact, the pull request and the posting of an approval | Hosting | the adapter that supplies the evidence from the bytes, mints the Approver's token with the abilities declared, calls the approve endpoint and refuses a token without the ability |
| what makes a SYNC clean, and `verify Sync` and `approve Sync` as controls | Release | the check at the head and the wiring that asks the Approver |
| the signed check | the Inspector, with Controls | one check per registered control, signed at a revision |
| creating and installing the App, storing its key, drawing its logo | the Owner, by hand | nothing in the repository |

`judgeApproval(evidence, expected)` takes the revision and base being decided, the revision of main and the trusted triggers as `expected` and the evidence as data, and answers `{ decision: 'approve' | 'abstain', findings }`. It does not fetch the pull request, does not read the check from GitHub and does not post: Hosting gives it the evidence and acts on the answer, and a later push moves the head, so an approval already given is dismissed by the ruleset and the Approver is asked again for the new head. The CTO's call is that the Approver's workflow executes only code from main, on a trusted trigger, never checks out the head and judges from facts; `judgeApproval` takes the facts of that execution as evidence and abstains where they are missing. The workflow itself and its proof at WIRE are not in this Change. None of it is shipped in the Skill, which stays free of KAAL's own Apps.
