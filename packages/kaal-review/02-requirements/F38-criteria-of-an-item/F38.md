# review/F38 criteria-of-an-item

The criteria of an item are the dimensions and rules by which the comparison of its scope with its reference is judged, for instance completeness, consistency, testability or being free of overlap. They are the Criteria chapter of the item, a list, and every item has one: an item with no Criteria is not valid, with or without a reference, and is a finding `item-incomplete` whose `missing` list names `criteria`; when the reference is missing too, the list names both. A criterion says how to judge and does not say what to judge against, so it does not restate or stand in for the reference. The script catches the plain case only: a criterion that is, word for word, an entry of `against` or of `validates` is a finding named `criterion-restates-reference`, and the item stays listed. Whether a criterion is a dimension of judgement and not a disguised reference is the appraiser's judgement and is not scripted. Criteria do not carry the reference when `against` is missing: an item with Criteria and no reference is not valid.

**Done when** an item with a reference and criteria is valid, an item with criteria and no reference and an item with a reference and no criteria are each not valid and say which is missing, and a criterion that is plainly a reference entry is a named finding.
