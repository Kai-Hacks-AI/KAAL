# Process

A Process is a repeatable way of working, defined and owned by the capability whose purpose it serves. It is described through five further concepts, and none is inferred from another:

- **Activity**: a unit of work within a Process.
- **Role**: a responsibility within a Process, fulfilled by an actor: a human, a CASE Agent or another authorized participant. A Role is not an Agent, and one actor may fulfil several Roles.
- **State and Transition**: where something stands in a Process, and how it moves from one State to another. A State is derived from observable facts and is never kept as a claim of its own. Facts that contradict each other are a finding, and a missing fact leaves the State undecided, never assumed. Which States exist and which Transitions are allowed belong to the Process's owner.
- **Ordering**: how Activities relate to one another: in sequence, in parallel, as concerns with no order among them, or as a loop that repeats until its exit condition holds.
- **Entry and Exit conditions**: what must hold for a Process, or one of its Activities, to begin, and what must hold for it to end.

Describing a way of working as a Process authorizes nothing and makes nothing mandatory. Whether a Process applies, and how strictly, is decided where it runs.
